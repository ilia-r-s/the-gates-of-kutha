# The Gates of Kutha: The Complete Rulebook

Oct 24, 2026 · @Ilia

Every rule of The Gates of Kutha, build 26.1.0, with every probability: what you can do, what the camp, the cult and the power below do, what the dice decide, and how often each of the nineteen endings comes.

## The game in brief

You take over the British dig at Tell Ibrahim, ancient Kutha, in March 1926, five days before the dark of the moon. Seven people are in camp. Two of them serve one of the powers under the mound and are working a rite to open its gate; you have five days to find them, learn which power they serve and what binds it, and stop the rite.

**How a season ends.**

| Outcome | How | Odds, playing as a person would |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clean victory | The whole of the cult unmasked above ground (at evening inquiries, or caught at your tent by night); or, below, the rite stopped at the first try with nerve and a wound to spare; or the circle left with nobody to finish the rite | 2.3% |
| Hard victory | The rite stopped any other way: a later act, a shot, fire, a scarred rite, the rite breaking of itself | 7.3% |
| Defeat | The rite completes (doom), your wounds reach the limit (death), your nerve reaches nothing (madness), or the camp sends you away for the deed (sent away) | 90.3% (rite 23.9%, death 24.1%, madness 41.6%, sent away 0.7%) |

The odds are from 3,150 simulated seasons of build 26.1.0, bots playing the real page in seven styles (the full tables close this rulebook).

**How it plays.** A day has three slots (morning, afternoon, evening). In each you go somewhere and do one thing: talk to someone there, search the place, examine a fresh scene, translate a tablet fragment, rest, or, of an evening in camp, call an inquiry (or, once a season, settle it yourself). Each night the cult comes for someone, unless it lies low. Each dawn brings a trace at the scene, a sign of the power below, dreams, talk and news. On the fifth night, or as soon as the rite completes, you go down under the ziggurat: you name a power, choose up to two companions, pass seven gates and act in the sanctum.

**What you are never told.** The game never shows a probability, and never states nerve, wounds or trust as numbers in its text: losses and gains are told in words ("your nerve frays", "trusts you more now"). The meters at the top show nerve, wounds and the rite as a word each (The meters in words, below), and the casebook gives each person's trust as a word.

## Nothing is certain

Nothing in the game is decided with certainty: every answer, every action, everything that happens of itself, and how much it costs or gives back, is a throw of the dice. Often a near-certain one; never a sure one.

- **No single die is surer than 99% or rarer than 1%.** Odds of exactly nothing are kept for what cannot happen at all (nobody left to name, nothing to confess, no charm in the place).
- **Odds built from several things** (someone's trust, the camp's mood, your background) are kept between 5% and 95%. Only what is meant to be rare goes lower: a horror of its own accord early in the season, the wrong charm or the wrong name working all the same.
- **Amounts vary.** Nerve lost or regained, and each step the rite takes, come out one more or one less 25% of the time (half up, half down, never below nothing). A wound taken or healed, and fragments found by the rain or in a grave, vary the same way 10% of the time. A cost of nothing stays nothing.

**The rarest turns** are never announced; the ending tells them.

| Rare turn | Odds |
| --- | --- |
| A tablet line is a scribe's error, and false | 1% a line |
| A sign is not the power's at all, but another's | 1% a sign |
| One of a charm's marks is misread | 1% a charm |
| A habit you notice was only that day's, not theirs | 1% |
| A trace shows a habit not its owner's own that night (borrowed boots, a cadged cigarette) | 1% a trace |
| The old title a philologist knows is misremembered | 1% a season |

| A night brings the Dreamer no dream | 3% a night |

| An attack fails by luck | 3% |
| The scene of an attack is swept clean | 3% |
| The cult comes for you for no reason you will ever know | 1% a night, + 0.5% for each day gone |

**The seven exceptions**, each for a reason:

1. **The calendar.** Five days, three slots a day (two on a short day). It is the frame the season is played in.
2. **The counting.** The meters (nerve, wounds, the rite) and their limits (nerve at nothing is madness, wounds at the limit death, the rite at its end opens the gate), the word each meter shows and the casebook's word for each person's trust, the ending's score, and which ending a season comes to, which follows from what happened. What they show is exactly what stands; the chance is in what happens.
3. **A step of trust or suspicion is one step.** Both are short scales (trust from −2 to 3, suspicion from 0 to 3) and a step is their unit; whether anything moves is always a throw.
4. **The truth, once dealt.** The dice deal the parts, temperaments, likes and habits, the power below, its signs, titles and ward; after that they are simply what is true. What anyone says about them is a throw.
5. **The inquiry's verdict.** Once the camp backs an inquiry, the accused's true part comes out. As in Mafia, where a player put out shows their card, this anchors the deduction. Whether the camp backs you is a throw for every hand.
6. **The cult never confesses to being the cult.** It may tell the truth about other things, slip, flinch, blurt what only the attacker knew, or be named by a witness; its faith does not break of itself.
7. **Presentation.** Faces in the pictures, the casebook's words, the ending page's wording, the epilogues and the music are fixed by the season without the dice, so drawing or reading never changes what happens.

## The season

A season is five days of three slots (morning, afternoon, evening), each day followed by a night; the rite grows every night, and when it reaches 12 the gate opens.

- **Short days.** A shamal, or the strike the day after the camp walks out on your inquiries, leaves two slots: no evening at camp, and no inquiry that day.
- **The rite** starts at 1 of 12. Each night adds about 1.3 for each of the cult still at large (a step more or less 25% of the time), and up to 1.3 × as much against one who sits still (How the cult reads you, below); with nobody of it left at large it grows no more. Exposing one of the cult sets it back about 2; a wrong accusation advances it about 2. From 5 the scorched circle at the foot of the ziggurat is seen to widen on half the dawns; from 8 you are warned every dawn. When it completes, the descent begins at once.
- **Your nerve** starts at 10 (an occultist's at 8); at nothing you go mad. **Wounds**: the third kills you (an old soldier's fourth). A loss of two or more is told as a great one ("your nerve frays badly"), and so is a gain of three or more.
- **What brings nerve back**: rest in camp (about 2, a slot), brandy (about 3, no time, if you carry it), good news, now and then a friend's company, a companion at the top of the stair. A passing doctor may see to a wound. Exposing one of the cult gives no nerve back.

**Backgrounds**

| --- | --- | --- |

| Old soldier | Pressing +25%; examining a scene +10% (80%); carries a revolver; dies only at the fourth wound | None beyond the others' strengths |
| Occultist | Talking with one of the cult, a chill 30% of conversations (and a false chill beside an innocent 5%); knows one tablet line; below, a ward +10%, the counter-rite +15%, facing a traitor +15% | Nerve 8 |

**The casebook** is kept the way your reply to the Trustees' wire says, for the whole season. The usual way, it files what you learn under the person it concerns: it marks habits that turn up at a scene, says whom a trace or a sighting fits, flags accounts that disagree and two people claiming one part, and shows on each card who suspects that person in earnest and what has been said of them. Neither way does anything tell you how strong a case is. Played hardcore, it keeps only the bare record and joins nothing up; the rules are otherwise the same.

## The meters in words

The top panel shows nerve, wounds and the rite as a word each, never as a number, and never says where a limit lies: its tooltip says only what the limit means ("lose all of it and you go mad"). Each word covers a band, and its colour darkens as things get worse; the rite's words keep step with what the dawns say of it.

| Meter | Its words, from best to worst |
| --- | --- |
| Nerve | *steady* (at least 85% of the most you can hold), *uneasy* (65%), *frayed* (45%), *shaking* (less), *near breaking* (2 left), *breaking* (1 left) |

**The seven parts**, dealt before anything else in the season, by a fair shuffle of the cast and nothing else: any two of the seven may be the cult, each pair as likely as any other (1 in 21), and which of the two is the hierophant is picked at the same moment.

- **Two of the cult.** One is the hierophant. Either may claim a part that is not theirs, at the same odds as anyone (Claims in Crane's diary, below).
- **The Dreamer** dreams the truth about one person each night, the night Crane died among them (95% true, 5% muddied; 3% of nights no dream), never of the night's victim and never of the same person twice. In a true dream of the thief, 30% of the time the god's hand is on the thief's shoulder: a guilty dream of one who is no cultist.
- **The Watcher** watches over someone each night (you, the night they promised to, if they trust you).
- **The smuggler**, a thief, is out at night on business of his own.
- **Two innocents.**

Traces at a scene, sightings in the dark and what you notice in talk are all of these habits.

**Likes.** Each person likes each other person from −2 (cannot bear) to 2 (fond): −2 8%, −1 22%, 0 38%, 1 24%, 2 8%. A feeling is mutual 60% of the time, otherwise the other way is drawn on its own. The two of the cult lean toward each other (+1 each way, 60% of seasons, up to fond), which talk of friendships can give away. Wherever someone picks among people, likes weigh the pick × (1 + 0.45 × like).

**Trust toward you** runs from −2 (hostile) to 3 (devoted); the casebook shows it as hostile, strained, reserved, cordial, warm, devoted. It starts at 0 (70%), −1 (15%) or 1 (15%), then a step warmer at the temperament's warmth and a step cooler at its coolness (table above), never above fond (2) at the start. Talk alone never takes anyone past fond, or below hostile.

## The season's mood

Every season has a mood that sets how often things happen of themselves, strand by strand; it is dealt at the start (by weight: still and uneasy 3 each, the others 1.5 each, so 28.6% each for still and uneasy and 14.3% each for haunted, full of talk, full of comings and goings) and may turn at any dawn. Nothing names it: you feel it in the arrival, the nights and each first light, and the ending names the season's course.

Each strand runs at the rate below, × the usual chance of everything in it:

| Mood | Dread | Talk | News | Finds | Watch | Taken | Swing | Peril | Spite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Still | 0.55 | 0.35 | 0.35 | 0.7 | 1.35 | 0.85 | 0.85 | 1.7 | 1.2 |
| Uneasy | 1.1 | 0.8 | 0.6 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Haunted | 1.8 | 0.55 | 0.45 | 1.25 | 0.9 | 1.35 | 1.1 | 0.8 | 0.7 |
| Full of talk | 0.6 | 2 | 0.5 | 0.8 | 1.1 | 0.9 | 1.35 | 0.7 | 1.7 |
| Full of comings and goings | 0.75 | 0.7 | 2.2 | 0.9 | 0.8 | 1 | 1 | 1.3 | 0.8 |

- **Dread**: horrors of their own accord, horrors when you search (up to twice as often), and the uncanny among the news. **Talk**: the morning's talk, talk overheard, and more softly dreams told aloud, remarks in passing and flirtatious greetings; and the camp's own news (a quarrel, a feast, coffee with the headman, the diggers' song). **News**: the world outside. **Finds**: the finds tray. **Watch**: how much of people's habits you notice. **Taken**: how often the dead are the power's own work. **Swing**: how far the evening's mood swings at an inquiry. **Peril**: the dig's own dangers. **Spite**: harm done you in secret by night.
- Talk and news ramp up over the first days: × 0.7, 0.85, 1, 1, 1.1 on days 1 to 5.
- **Where dread runs above 1**, a second sign may come some mornings (25% × (rate − 1)), and a horror costs no nerve at all now and then, as you grow used to them (35% × (rate − 1)).
- **What comes seldom weighs more.** A happening of a strand running below 1 is heavy at 90% × (1 − rate): a horror one degree worse, news twice as strong, talk true to what the speaker knows and heard by more of the camp (× 1.5 onlookers moved).
- **Where talk or news runs above 1**, a second piece may come the same morning (30% × (rate − 1)).
- **Nothing is ruled out.** Wherever a strand's distance from 1 makes a chance, that distance is never taken as less than 0.02: a still season can still bring a second sign, a haunted one a heavy horror.
- **One thing brings another.** Each happening warms its strand (× 1.25, up to × 2; the warmth halves each dawn). Some news makes other news likelier (a dealer makes the inspector 3 × likelier, rain a grave 2 ×, a quarrel the diggers' song 2 ×, and so on), and a quarrel may flare up again at a later breakfast (40% a morning, once).
- **A morning holds only so much.** Beyond the night, the trace, the sign and grief for the dead, each extra the morning brings (a second sign, a find, a dream aloud, talk, news) multiplies the chance of every later one by 0.45 (by 0.75 when the mood favours the later one), and what the mood favours is told first.

**How the mood turns.** Each dawn it may turn at 15%, + 10% for each happening since the last dawn that drew it elsewhere and − 10% for each that held it, never above 70% or below 2%. What draws it: each happening toward its strand's mood (0.35); a murder toward talk (0.75); the power's own touch on the dead toward haunted (1); the rite stirring, each dawn from 5, toward haunted (0.5); an innocent sent away toward talk (1), an inquiry refused toward talk (0.5); one of the cult unmasked toward still (0.75); a day when nothing happened of itself toward still (0.5). It turns into a neighbour, by weight × (1 + whatever drew it there); a pull toward a mood that is no neighbour counts toward uneasy. A morning tells of a turn 70% of the time.

| From | Into, by weight |
| --- | --- |
| Still | uneasy 2.5, haunted 1, comings and goings 1, talk 0.6 |
| Uneasy | still 1, haunted 1.5, talk 1.5, comings and goings 1 |
| Haunted | uneasy 2, still 0.8, talk 0.8, comings and goings 0.4 |
| Full of talk | uneasy 2, still 0.5, comings and goings 1, haunted 0.7 |
| Full of comings and goings | uneasy 2, still 1, talk 1, haunted 0.5 |

## A day

Each slot you choose a place and do one thing there; everyone else has gone somewhere by the place weights below, and the site plan shows their initials.

**Where people go.** By weight: camp 3, Trench B 3, the finds magazine 2, the workmen's village 2, the canal 1.5, the ziggurat 1, the Parthian graves 1 (so 22%, 22%, 15%, 15%, 11%, 7%, 7%). In the evening the camp weighs 2 more, and the cult goes to the ziggurat (+3) and the graves (+2). Someone avoids a place where one they cannot bear already is (80%), and goes where a fond friend is (25%); one of the cult mostly will not follow its partner (85%), the better not to be seen together.

**What a slot can hold**

- **Talk** to someone there (Conversations, below).
- **Search** the place (not the camp). What turns up is drawn by the place's weights; a horror's weight is × the season's dread (up to × 2). An object is one you do not carry yet (an incantation bowl, a flask of brandy, an electric torch, a service revolver), else a fragment. A charm hidden there turns up 85% of the time on each search, so it may take another; after a charm the same hole gives up something more 50% of the time (nothing and horrors then weigh 0.3 × as much). A fragment comes in two pieces 8% of the time. A horror from searching costs 1 nerve, 2 half the time.

| Trench B | 45% | 15% | 15% | 15% | 10% |
| The ziggurat E-meslam | 20% | 35% | 15% | 25% | 5% |

- **Examine** the fresh scene of the night's attack: it reads 70% of the time (an old soldier 80%). Read, it tells what the trace is: the attacker's (and 60% of the time a second habit of theirs beside it, unless the attacker was tidy; a careless attacker's two traces are both shown genuine and may give up a third); a thief's; an older trace left by someone there earlier; or an innocent's laid by the cult, shown planted, with the attacker's own beneath it half the time. The scene misleads you 15% of the time (3% when two traces lie side by side): the attacker's trace taken for an older one, or an older one for the attacker's. A scene that will not read keeps something for another look 20% of the time. Whatever turns up belongs to the night of the attack.
- **Translate** a fragment at camp. It goes 62% of the time (a philologist 77%) and gives about four lines (now and then three or five; a philologist one more half the time). You may look for something you have met (a sign you have seen, a charm you carry, an old title): the first line is about it 80% of the time and a second 50%; the rest lean 60% toward what you know. One line in a hundred is a scribe's error, and false. Reading costs nerve, the more the more you already know: 1 nerve at 50% + 3% for each line you knew already (at most 90%; a philologist × 0.75), and then 2 instead of 1 at 20% + 2% per line known. A failed translation costs 1 nerve 40% of the time and crumbles the fragment 30%. At night the lines may come back to you: 1.2% per line known (at most 40%), for 1 nerve.
- **Rest** in camp: about 2 nerve back. **Brandy**, if you carry it: about 3, and takes no time.
- **Call an inquiry**, at camp, of an evening, once a day (The inquiry, below); or, once a season from the third day, settle it yourself instead (Settling it yourself, below).

**After anything you do** you may notice a habit of someone there (35% × the season's watch; 1% of the time a habit of that day only, not theirs) or overhear gossip (25% × the season's talk).

At night a horror of the night may come at the same chance, × 0.3 on a night that came for you. None of them is a sign of any power.

## Conversations

A conversation is two questions, then you take your leave (8% of the time a third; else 8% of the time only one), and the page says so in words: after the second question, that they are in no hurry today; after the first, that they have little time today. Backing out before the first question costs nothing. What you say, and much of what is said back, comes in several tellings, gone through in turn without the dice, so that nothing is said the same way twice running. Anyone may be having a bad day and keep it short, refuse a favour or not name names: 5% × how readily their feelings show (× 0.4 with a friend). Someone at trust −1 or lower keeps it short 60% of the time (× how readily it shows), refuses a favour 60%, will not name names 50%.

**Whom they name.** Of the innocent: whom they suspect most, if anyone in earnest (60%; a fond friend only at the strongest suspicion); else a real cultist at the temperament's rate (20% to 45%; the smuggler +15%); else whomever they dislike most (hardly ever, 1%, even a fond friend). The honest and the conscientious may name the thief for what he is (10%). The thief who saw an attack names that one outright 10% of the time, else hints 60%. One of the cult points at the thief (the honest one 70%, else at its strategic rate), gives up its own partner once that one looks lost (10% × its readiness to sacrifice), or names an innocent at its frame rate: whoever accuses it, if calculating, else one it dislikes. An honest cultist hardly ever names an innocent.

**How feelings show.** Whether a feeling shows depends on its strength (warmth at trust 1, 2, 3: 30%, 70%, 90%; coldness at −1, −2: 55%, 85%), × how readily their temperament shows it (honest 1.15, light-headed 1.2, suspicious 1, conscientious 0.9, liar 0.85, calculating 0.6, detached 0.5). Anyone has a good or bad day that has nothing to do with you (10%). A feeling held back may show in the face all the same (25%). One of the cult courting your trust may put on a warm face it does not feel (honest 1%, detached 10%, conscientious and suspicious 15%, light-headed 20%, liar 35%, calculating 45%). As you leave: a lingering look from one sweet on you (20%), a warm word from a friend (20%), a back turned by one against you (30%), a parting glimpse of their manner (15%), each only if it shows. The casebook's word for their trust is always exact.

## The night

Each night the cult comes for one person, as a rule someone other than you, unless it lies low, and the rite grows by about 1.3 for each of the cult still at large; then the night may bring a horror, the words you have read, or harm in secret.

The night's page is told in parts, each under its own heading: *In the night* (how the evening ended, the night itself, your own dream if one comes), *At first light* (the dead and their trace, the camp's grief, the sign, the scorched circle, the mood's turning), *At breakfast* (dreams and the morning's talk) and *The day's news*. A part with nothing in it is not told; on the night the rite completes there is only *Before dawn*.

**Whom the cult comes for**, in this order:

1. **No one**, when it lies low: 4% × the season's patience (dealt unseen with the season: 0.5, 1, 1.6 or 2.5, by weights 1, 2, 1.5, 1), × (1 + 0.35 for each of the camp who suspects in earnest the one of it most suspected), × 3 the night after one of it was unmasked, × 2 once the rite has reached 8; × 0.4 while someone the camp has heard dream one of it guilty is alive, since that cannot wait; × 0.5 for each such night already; ÷ how far it reads you as sitting still (How the cult reads you, below); never above 40%. The morning finds everyone there, and nobody believes it; the rite grows all the same. The ending says why each quiet night was quiet, when it had a reason: the camp closing in on one of it, one of it unmasked that evening, or the rite far enough along to risk nothing.
2. **The power below, by itself**, once nobody of the cult is left at large (the last of it dead by your own hand: Settling it yourself, below). The rite grows no more, but the power comes 85% of nights, for whom the cult would have chosen and as the cult would have come (the Watcher and luck may turn it as they would turn anyone), so that the night tells nothing of who is left. Its scene holds what any scene may (the thief's trace, or that of someone who had been there earlier: anyone's, if nobody was about), but nothing of an attacker's own, and nobody saw anyone at it. The ending tells it.
3. **You**, if you confided in one of the cult (55%), if one of it has turned against you (trust −1 or lower: 20%), or for no reason you will ever know (1% + 0.5% for each day gone); the cult comes the readier against one who leans on inquiries, the tablets or the ground (How the cult reads you, below). On a night one of it swore to watch your tent, the first two count only half (The cult's craft, below).
4. **One of its own**, rarely: when one of the cult is all but caught (a strong case, two of the camp suspecting it in earnest, or an inquiry refused against it), the other may kill it, at 40% × its readiness to sacrifice its own (5% to 55%), × 0.3 on the first night, × 0.3 again if the killer is under any suspicion itself. It is told as any murder, and only the ending tells it for what it was.
5. **The dreamer the camp has heard name one of it guilty** most often (70%: the true Dreamer, as a rule, since it goes by what was dreamt, not by who dreams true).
6. **The last pretender to dream aloud** at breakfast (50%, × 0.3 while those dreams have done it no harm: a pretender naming the innocent does its work for it).
7. **Someone who has named an innocent guilty before everyone**, in a dream or in blame aloud (60% × the best of it at planting a trace), so that with them dead it is the one they named whom the camp looks at.
8. **Anyone else**, by weight: 1 + 0.25 per step of their trust in you (the more once it reads you as one who leans on friends); × (1 − 0.35 × like) for each of the cult, by how much it likes them; × (1 + 0.25 × suspicion) for each of the cult they have begun to suspect; × (1 − 0.12 × how many of the camp suspect them in earnest), since a scapegoat is worth more alive; never below 0.1.

The night takes anyone but you in Trench B, on the ziggurat, among the graves, by the canal or in the magazine (weight 1 each) or in the village (0.3); you, in your tent.

**The Watcher** watches over you the night they promised to, if they trust you (2 or more), 90% of the time; a promise is for that night only, the true Watcher's as anyone's, and another day needs another promise. Otherwise the Watcher watches over anyone at random, you and the cult included: the Watcher cannot tell. The Watcher there drives off whoever comes 90% of the time, and is seen doing it at your tent 90% of the time. Someone unwatched may yet be spared by luck (3%: the dogs, a light, a sleeper stirring).

**An attack on you** is turned, in this order, by the Watcher (above); by the incantation bowl if you carry it (it holds 90% of the time, and breaks as it is used 90% of the time whether it held or not); by the revolver if you carry it (40%: about a nerve); by waking in time (3%: about a nerve); otherwise you lose about 2 nerve and take a wound, in the power's own manner 97% of the time.

**The dead.** A death shakes you (about a nerve) at 55% + 15% per step of trust the dead had in you. They are found where it happened, by someone still at the dig, in one of seven ways. They are the power's own work at (15% + 4% per day gone + 20% × the rite's progress) × the season's taken, never above 45%: one of twelve states of the body in the power's own colour. Otherwise the cult killed them by hand: one of eight ways anywhere, or (40%, where the place has its own) one of two ways only that place allows, with the power's mark on the body 90% of the time (one of three per nature). The cult signs its work besides 30% of the time (seven signatures). A death is in the power's own manner 97% of the time, else another power's of the season. No state of the body is told twice while another is left. Crane, the night before you came, was the power's own work 70% of the time, otherwise the hierophant's, at the foot of the ziggurat; his scene and everyone's account of that night are made like any other night's.

**The trace at the scene.** 3% of the time the ground is swept and there is nothing to examine. Otherwise, in this order: the smuggler's own trace, come to rob the place (25%; 60% if he swore a watch and was out); else, if anyone else was about there that day or night, theirs, from earlier (55%); else an innocent's laid by the attacker (its craft: plant), or the attacker's own. A careless attacker leaves a second trace of its own. Each trace is one habit, of three, drawn at random; 1% of traces show a habit not their owner's own that night.

**Everyone's account.** Every night, attack or none, everyone is somewhere: the Watcher in camp; the attacker where it happened and its partner on the ziggurat (the graves, if it happened there); the smuggler in camp 15% of the time, else the magazine or the canal, half and half; the innocent by weight (camp 5, village 2, magazine 1, canal 1, trench 0.4, ziggurat 0.4, graves 0.3); one who spent the night in your tent, there, by her own account (A visitor at night, below). Then:

- Two who are sweet on each other, if there is such a pair, slip out together somewhere quiet, never where it happened (30% of nights), and each but the honest swears they were asleep in camp (75%); a priest keeps his vows.
- Friends spend the evening together (60%), wherever anyone might be of an evening, but never where it happened out on the tell.
- Someone out alone was where it happened by ill luck (20%).
- The innocent tell the truth about their evening, except: anyone will not say (3%); one out alone says they were in camp, out of fear, at (1 − their truthfulness) × 75% if they were where it happened, × 8% otherwise, and names a friend as company 45% of the time (the friend backs it: one of the cult 70%, a fond friend who bends the truth 50%, one who merely likes them 15%, anyone else only by misremembering); habitual liars lie about small things at their rate; anyone alone in camp may say they could not sleep (15%); the Watcher hiding the part says no more than the truth (the honest 99%, others 50%).
- The thief says he was in his tent; he tells the truth 5% of the time (an honest thief 20%, else he will not say) and will not say 10%; he lets fall what he saw 5%.
- One of the cult claims an evening with someone who was alone (38%, so that the cult claims company as often as anyone does), where that one truly was (never the Watcher, nor anyone alone where it happened), whom a fond friend may back; or early to bed. The attacker now and then owns being out near the place (20%).
- **Sightings.** Each innocent saw someone pass in the dark (out: 50%; from camp: 16%) and noticed one habit: the thief, if out where they were (60%); else the attacker, or someone else about there by ill luck (weight 0.8 each against the attacker's 1). The light-headed misremember the habit at their rate. One of the cult invents a sighting of an innocent it dislikes at 72% × its frame rate; an innocent sure of someone's guilt, or who cannot bear them, may say they saw them (12% × frame). One attacked and saved glimpsed a habit of the attacker's (15%). A friend (trust 2 or more) who saw someone may tell you before breakfast, unasked (35%).

**The thief as witness.** Out on his business, the smuggler sees the attack 8% of nights, 50% if he was where it happened, 40% if his trace is at the scene (never in camp, never if he was the one attacked). The one he saw is the one who did it 95% of the time, else someone else about in the dark. He says nothing (a thief who saw a murder would be next), but asked whom he distrusts he names them outright 10% of the time or hints 60%, hints in gossip (50%), says it aloud at breakfast now and then (10%), and at an inquiry votes against them as one who knows. When his own story breaks he names them, if they are still at large: pressed into owning the smuggling (90%), coming clean of his own accord (60%), sent away by an inquiry (80%). Having named one, he would name another he saw 30% of the time.

**Harm in secret.** Someone who cannot stand you (trust −1 or lower, the cult included) may harm you in secret: at most one a night, at 7% × 2 at trust −2 × their temperament (suspicious 1.4, liar 1.3, calculating 1, light-headed 0.7, detached 0.5, conscientious 0.3, honest 0.01) × the season's spite; × 0.3 on a night the cult came for you, or one that brought both a horror and the words. A scorpion in your boot (a wound), salt in your water (2 nerve), your lamp oil watered (a nerve), or a brandy, the torch or the revolver gone from your kit. It lands 70% of the time; otherwise you find it in time. You catch them at it 20% of the time (it counts against them at an inquiry; in the grey light you take them for someone else 5%), else they may leave a trace of themselves by your tent (35%).

**The words.** The lines you have read may come back in the night (1.2% per line known, at most 40%), for a nerve. A horror of the night may come too (A day, above).

**Onto them.** If the one the night took had named someone still at the dig guilty before everyone, someone may say so aloud at first light, as half the camp is thinking it (45%), the likelier the more they suspect the one named. It may be what the cult killed them for, and it may be what it wanted the camp to think; the line is the same either way. It is a public moment (The camp, below).

**A watch sworn, and told of.** The morning after someone swore to watch your tent (one of the cult, a pretender, or the true Watcher who watched it that night as promised) and nothing came for you, the one who swore may tell the camp at breakfast that they saw someone off in the small hours (30%, as the morning has room): the cult, to be trusted for it; anyone else, to be thanked, or because they heard something in the dark and believe it. Whoever doubted them may think again. Nothing had come near your tent; the ending tells it.

## A visitor at night

Once a season at most, from day 2, a woman with a soft spot for you who has come to trust you (2 or more) may come to your tent as the evening ends: afraid, she says, that they may not see the light of day, and not wanting to spend the night alone. She comes at 4% + 2% for each of the night's dead so far (at most 15%), the fonder of you the likelier, and never on the night you go out yourself. An occultist may feel the cult's cold as she comes in (30% if she is of the cult), or a cold that is only the night (5%).

- **Sent back**, she trusts you a step less (90%; a second step less 30% of those times), and her friends may think the less of you for it (20% × liking).
- **She stays**, and if she is of the cult she may do the cult's work herself in the small hours (30%, never on a night the cult lies low), and mostly she does it (90%): the season ends. If not, you catch her wrist in time, for about 2 nerve and a wound, and by first light she is in the truck to Hillah as one of them unmasked: the rite falls back about 2, and if she was the last of them the season is won, all but always, as at an inquiry.
- **Otherwise the night goes as any night.** The cult coming for her knows where she sleeps tonight half the time: then it comes to your tent, past the Watcher if it is watched and past luck, and she dies beside you in your sleep (about 2 nerve); else it finds her own tent empty, nobody dies, and by breakfast the camp has worked out where she slept. Coming for you, past the Watcher, the bowl, the revolver and luck, it finds two of you: it takes her (40%), you (25%) or both (15%), or does only the usual harm as she screams and it flees (20%). The last of the cult in your tent, who let you be, does not come for you that night. One of the cult in your tent may slip out to do the night's work herself; she says she was in camp, as any guest would.
- **A death in your tent** is talked of hardly (25%), readily (50%) or wildly (25%). Talked of at all, her friends (20% × liking) and now and then anyone (5%) think the less of you, twice as readily when it is talked of wildly; talked of wildly, the camp's suspicion of you rises a step (it weighs only if you go out yourself: Settling it yourself, below).
- **The morning after**, if you both live, she is gone before you wake (45%) or still there, whatever she is: one who slipped out on the cult's work is back by then as often as anyone else stays. Your nerve may mend (60%: about 1), and she may trust you the more (50%). The camp talks twice as readily: a friend of hers takes it well or badly (20% × liking), one taken with her badly (50%). A flirtation that came to this is likelier to come to more (Flirtation, The camp, below).

Nothing of it is ever explained; the ending tells what she was, and what the night held.

## Settling it yourself

Once a season, of an evening at camp, you may settle it yourself instead of calling an inquiry: from the third day, even once the camp will hold no more inquiries, but never on the last evening, whose night belongs to the dark of the moon ("There is only one way to stop this"). You choose whom (your mark beside each name, never how strong the case looks), with what (your revolver, if you carry it: sure, and loud; or a knife from the cook-house: quiet, if your hand is steady), and whether to leave someone else's habit by the tent, as you have noted it, so that a habit noted wrongly points at whoever truly has it, or at nobody. The evening is spent, and there is no inquiry. Nothing ever tells you whether you were right, but the ending.

The night goes as any night, by its own dice. In the small hours, after the cult has been, you go:

- **Away.** Whoever you went for is not in their tent, about as often whoever they are: dead already, at the cult's hands; the one of the cult who did the night's work, not back yet (10%); the thief, still out at work (8%); two out together somewhere quiet (60%); or anyone, up and about at that hour (8%). About a nerve.
- **Watched.** The Watcher over them that night, if still alive, drives you off (90%) and may know you (60%). About 2 nerve.
- **Done**, at 60% with the knife and 85% with the revolver (an old soldier +10%). They are found at first light, dead in their cot, and mourned like any of the night's dead. About 3 nerve.
- **Fought off.** Otherwise they wake and fight you off, and may have seen your face (45%). About 2 nerve and a wound; if that wound is your last, the ending tells it.

Someone may pass you between the tents: 18% with the knife, 36% after a shot, 9% if you never reached the cot; if the Watcher was watching your tent, it is the Watcher. Having reached the cot, you may leave a trace of your own without knowing it (30%: a horn button), besides any habit you laid. If the cult came for you that night, it found your tent empty. If it was the last of the cult you killed, you carry on in the dark, none the wiser: the power below still comes by night (The night, above), and the descent finds the circle empty.

**The morning after**, whoever knows it was you (the one who woke, or the Watcher who drove you off, if they knew you; or whoever passed you) may tell the camp before breakfast: 60% if they trust you −1 or less, 35% at 0, 15% at 1, 5% from a friend; +5% from the cult; +25% if they saw your face. Else they hint at it (60%), else they keep it to themselves. The camp's suspicion of you runs from 0 to 3, and rises a step for each thing it has against you: a hint; your button, linked to you (40%); your tent found empty the night they came for you; a shot you fired, laid at your door (35%); the habit you laid seen for what it was (30%) and linked to you (30%). A habit not seen through is taken for its owner's, and the camp looks at them (A habit by the tent, The camp, below). If whoever you went for was away, nothing comes of it but, at most, a hint (half a step). Otherwise the cult, whoever you went for, learns it was you (50%; × 0.4 if nobody told, hinted or tied your button to you), and then knows what you are, as if you had confided in it. The camp talks twice as readily that morning.

**The camp's judgement.** Told it was you, the camp judges you at once; suspecting you at 2 or more, it does so that morning at 45% (75% at 3), and at each later dawn at half that. Each of the camp leans by its suspicion of you (+1 if someone told), +1 for a friend of the one you went for, +3 for the one you went for, if alive, − their trust in you, + 0.15 × the night's dead so far, + 0.25 × the camp's anger, + 0.3 × its push rate for the cult, ± the evening's mood and their own whim, as at an inquiry. At 2.2 or more they would send you away; below 0.6 they speak for you; between, they say nothing. With at least 2 for sending you away, and more of them than speak for you, you are put on the truck to Hillah, and the season ends (*The Truck to Hillah*). Otherwise you stay: the camp's anger rises by 1, its suspicion of you falls a step, and those who would have sent you away trust you less.

The ending counts the deed against you, and a killing names your style (Endings, below).

## Your own dreams

**Spoiler: the game never explains any of this to the player.**

Now and then you dream yourself. Each season deals, unseen, how often a night brings you a dream (never, at weight 2.5; 15%, at 2; 30%, at 2.5; 45%, at 2: so 28% of seasons bring none) and how often a dream is true (45%, at weight 1.5; 60%, at 2; 75%, at 2; 90%, at 1.5). A dream comes in the night, told before anything the night has done is known, and goes into your journal. It is of one kind, by weight (a kind that cannot be dreamt that night is passed over):

- **The gates** (2.5): someone still at the dig stands inside the seventh gate with the god's hand on their shoulder, or outside, weeping; now and then (12%) it is yourself.
- **Whom the night comes for** (1): someone, you, or nobody, tonight. A false one shows someone when nobody is coming (or you, 25%), and, when someone is, someone else (70%) or nobody.
- **Who cares for whom** (2): two of the camp, as they feel about each other; false, with one of the two feelings turned about.
- **A habit at an old scene** (1.5): the night of an attack gone by, and one habit left there: the attacker's, or one the attacker does not have.
- **A part someone keeps** (1): the Dreamer's, the Watcher's or the thief's (one someone at the dig still keeps), and who keeps it.
- **Whom someone lies awake for** (1): one who suspects another in earnest, or, false, one who does not (only once some of the camp do and some do not).
- **A line of the tablets** (0.7): read in the dream, as written, or changed as a scribe's error would change it; a line that was a scribe's error is false however it is dreamt.
- **The door** (0.4): from day 3, the door at the foot of the stair, standing open on the night the moon is dark, if nothing stops it first. It is only ever of what is sure, and always counted true.

Nothing says which dreams are true. The ending lists each dream, and whether it was so; a season without them says nothing of them.

## The morning

Each dawn tells the night, then brings a sign of the power below as a rule, and perhaps a find, dreams, talk and news, each extra less likely for every one already told (The season's mood, above).

**In order.** The mood may turn. A strike or a second day of dust is told. The camp's anger cools (The inquiry, below) and a camp that walked out may relent. Then the night: who was attacked and what became of them, the trace, perhaps a horror of the night, the scorched circle (from rite 5, half the dawns; from 8, every dawn). Then:

- **The sign**, 95% of dawns: one not yet seen as a rule (an old one at 0.05 of the weight), told in one of three ways, the ways not yet heard first. 1% of signs are not the power's at all but another's. Where dread runs high, a second sign may follow.
- **The finds tray**, 35% × the season's finds: a charm you have not found, brought in by Hamid (or a pickman, if Hamid is gone).
- **Dreams.** The true Dreamer, unless hiding the gift, brings you the night's dream before breakfast if they trust you or promised (90%), else may tell it aloud at breakfast (35% × room × the talk's softness, 0.6 + 0.4 × the talk rate, between 0.6 and 1.4). Anyone who only claims to dream, the cult as readily, dreams aloud of their own accord (25% × room × softness), and the last of them to do so is the loud one (The night, above). Dreams aloud are told in shuffled order, so who spoke first tells you nothing, and each may draw an answer (Claims, below).
- **The camp's talk**: rumours of what you did (twice as readily the morning after a night visit or the deed); grief for the night's dead (Public moments, below), and whoever liked the dead mourns them (90%), the one who liked them most the likeliest to take it hardest; the camp settles what was said of the dead as of an innocent, unless two suspected them in earnest (then 25% of the time it will not), and someone may say aloud whom the dead had named (Onto them, The night, above); a watch told of (The night, above); a friend's sighting; perhaps one person comes clean (Coming clean, below), the cult's false claims among them; perhaps an old quarrel flares up again. Then, at 60% × the talk rate, one of: talk about you (weight 0.35), gossip (0.45) or blame aloud (0.2); where talk runs high, a second of another kind.
- **News**, one piece at most as a rule, by strand: the world outside 24%, the uncanny 10%, the camp's own 10% of mornings, each × its strand's rate and the room left. A piece already told comes again only at 0.1 of its weight. Some come only when they can: the inspector needs a fragment to take, the Museum's cable a line you have not read, a shamal a full day; the voice on the wireless, before the rite stirs (5), comes at 0.2 of its weight.

## Claims in Crane's diary, and dreams told aloud

Crane's diary says one of the camp dreams true and one keeps watch; each person decides once, as the season is dealt, what part to claim, by part and temperament and nothing else. The cult claims at the same odds as anyone, so a claim alone tells nothing of who makes it.

**Who claims what** (temperament rates in The cast, above):

- **The Dreamer and the Watcher** hide the part at their temperament's rate, afraid of the knife or because a watcher works better unseen; hiding, the honest will not answer (99% of the time), others claim no part. A hidden part comes out at trust 3 (80% × (1 − half their hiding rate), once a day), under pressure, or of their own accord (Coming clean, below).
- **The smuggler** may claim the watch, to cover his nights out, at the temperament's cover rate.
- **Everyone else**, the cult and the innocent alike (and a smuggler with no cover), claims a part that is not theirs at the temperament's rate (1% for the honest to 75% for the light-headed), the Dreamer's or the Watcher's at even odds: the cult as cover, anyone else to be listened to.
- **Some will not say.** One with nothing to claim will not answer at all 3% of the time (the honest 15%), whoever they are.
- **Late claims.** One who has said nothing of the diary yet and begins to look lost (a case of 1.25 or more, or two of the camp suspecting them in earnest) may claim a part they never spoke of, at 12% × that rate a dawn: the cult to cover itself, anyone else in fear. Nobody hears of it until they say it. One named guilty in a dream told aloud may claim to dream on the spot (below).

**Made-up dreams.** Whoever only says they dream makes a dream up once a day, and tells the same one again that day (at breakfast, asked whom they distrust, or as you take your leave). A pretender's names someone guilty 40% of the time: someone they dislike or suspect, dreaming their own fears, or, once another has dreamt aloud, that rival 30% of the time; otherwise it clears a friend. One of the cult who claims to dream weighs what a dream is for: it gives up the other of it once that one looks lost (10% × its readiness to sacrifice its own: a true dream); clears the other of it once it is watched, by anyone suspecting it in earnest or naming it guilty aloud (80% × its readiness to defend its own: a lie); names whoever is closing in on it guilty (90% × its frame rate: a lie); and otherwise dreams as a pretender would, guilty 44% of the time, a guilty dream naming the other of it, while that one is not watched, at 55% × (½ + its readiness to sacrifice), to stand apart from it at no cost. So the cult's dreams, taken together, look like anyone's. A Dreamer who trusts you tells you a dream as you take your leave (60%); one who only claims to dream does so 40% of the time if they promised you their dreams, 10% if not.

**A watch sworn over your tent and slept through** is owned up to, denied, or not spoken of, by temperament:

| Temperament | Owns it | Denies it | Says nothing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Honest | 95% | 1% | 4% |
| Conscientious | 80% | 5% | 15% |
| Light-headed | 75% | 10% | 15% |
| Calculating | 45% | 35% | 20% |
| Detached | 45% | 10% | 45% |
| Suspicious | 40% | 35% | 25% |
| A liar | 25% | 55% | 20% |

**What a guilty dream told at breakfast draws**, there and then:

- **For one they would protect.** Anyone else who claims to dream may answer for the one named with a clean dream of their own ("Then we did not dream the same dream"): for a friend, or, in the cult, for the other of it; at 35% × their temperament's readiness to defend (25% to 80%). The true Dreamer answers so only with a clean dream of them to tell, anyone else only with no dream told yet that day. Nobody answers for themselves this way.
- **A dream in answer.** The one named may answer with a dream naming the dreamer, the oldest trick there is: one who already claims to dream at 10% (their first dream aloud) or 30% (already dreaming aloud), × their temperament (light-headed 1.4, liar 1.3, suspicious 0.99, conscientious and detached 0.3, calculating 0.2, honest 0.01), × 2 once they look lost anyway; one who claimed no part, the cult as anyone but never the Watcher, may even claim to dream on the spot (4% × that temperament factor), a pretender from then on. The true Dreamer, named guilty, answers with what they dreamt of their accuser, if they dreamt them guilty (50%). So an answer proves nothing by itself; the camp distrusts it all the same (Public moments, below).
- **"A lie," says the one named.** Otherwise they may call it a lie before everyone (30% × their temperament: suspicious 1.6, light-headed 1.3, liar 1.1, honest and conscientious 0.8, calculating 0.5, detached 0.4), in anger, coldly or plainly as their temperament has it (75%; else in another way), whatever they are.

Measured over 700 seasons of build 26, a false claim to dream, once stated, came from the cult 51% of the time, from an innocent 38% and from the thief 10%.

## The camp

Everyone keeps their own count of whom they suspect, from 0 to 3 (begins to wonder, suspects in earnest, all but sure), apart from whom they like; it moves a step at a time, mostly at public moments.

**Public moments.** Some things happen before the whole camp. Every onlooker (everyone at the dig except whoever started it and whoever it is about) gets one roll and is moved one step or not at all, at most three people each way per moment. A heavy moment (one that comes seldom in the season's mood) moves each onlooker 1.5 × as readily. The one who speaks up at a moment moves a step in their trust in you 90% of the time.

**The truth settles what was said.** When someone's part comes out, as the camp sees it (exposed, sent away, or taken by the night, whom the camp takes for innocent), each speaker's last word about them is proven right or wrong before the camp and remembered: it makes their next dream or accusation more, or less, believable.

**What you do gets round.** At most three of your deeds are talked of a morning (each deed to each person once). Pressed someone: each of their friends cools a step at 30% × how much they like them, and each who dislikes them warms at 15%. Confided in someone: each of their friends warms at 20% × like.

**What is said of you.** At breakfast someone may talk about you, the likelier the more strongly they feel (weight |trust| + 0.5): as they feel, mostly, the other way 15% of the time (the indifferent either way); a calculating one of the cult talks you down on purpose, 60% of the time when indifferent and 30% even behind a warm face (trust 1 or more). Their friends listen: bad talk 40% × like, good 35% × like.

**Gossip** (at breakfast, in conversation, overheard) is about a temperament (65%) or a friendship or feud, true at the speaker's rate (35% to 95%), 8% less true per step they feel about the one discussed, and when false slanted toward the bad for someone disliked (and by the suspicious) and toward the good for a friend. When gossip about a bond is false, a real friendship or feud is told backwards 50% of the time (else nothing is said), and where there is none, one is invented 30% of the time.

**Coming clean.** Someone keeping up a pretence (a part claimed that is not theirs, the cult's among them; a watch claimed as cover; a true part hidden; the smuggling) may drop it any morning of their own accord, at their temperament's daily rate (3% to 45%), + 20% if another claims the same part, + 15% if a dream named them, + 30% if a sworn watch failed, + 10% if two or more distrust them, + 8% if they trust you (2+). A hidden part comes out at half the temperament's rate + 12% the morning after a death + 8% if they trust you; the smuggling at 0.3 × the temperament's rate. Never above 75% a day, so some keep a lie to the end. 40% of the time it is said to you alone before breakfast, otherwise before everyone. At an inquiry, with the camp watching, 15% more. Pressed and shaken, anyone gives up a story they have told at 45% + their temperament's rate; trust 3 brings a hidden part out (80% × (1 − half their hiding rate)). The cult never comes clean as the cult; a false part it gives up as anyone does, at the same odds and in the same words, and the lie underneath goes on. A liar or a calculating one who told you they had no part (never the smuggler) may stage a coming clean as the Watcher (2% a morning), whoever they are: a new lie in the very words of a true confession. Said to you alone by one who trusts you (2 or more), it comes with a promise to watch your tent that night, a false watch (A sworn watch, The cult's craft, below). Pressing never brings a staged confession out; the ending tells it.

**Flirtation.** Dealt at the start: each woman (Constance, Ilse) may have a soft spot for you (40%), and anyone may for a particular person of the other sex (25%); Père Anselm keeps his vows, and the text never says whether you are a man or a woman. It shows only while the feeling holds (her trust in you 2 or more; their liking 1 or more), and only as far as manner lets it: a greeting (35%), an answer with a flirtatious look (25%), a look as you leave (20%), a word let slip when they talk of the other (50%; 2% of the time of one they are not taken with), a word about the one they are taken with (60% of such words), a piece of gossip (15%; made up 30% × how untrue the speaker's gossip runs). Beyond a tryst of a night and the night visit (A visitor at night, above), it changes nothing in play. In the epilogue it may come to more, decided without the dice: two of the camp who both left free, one taken with the other who at least likes them back, at 30% (half if one-sided), +20% if fond of each other, +10% if neither suspected the other, −25% if either did in earnest; a woman with a soft spot for you, 25% at her trust 2, 50% at 3, +25% if she spent a night in your tent (and then she comes first), otherwise she writes. Someone taken with one the night took may mourn them (60%). No one has two romances, and none with the priest.

## The cult's craft

The cult is clever, mostly, and how clever depends on each cultist's temperament:

| Temperament | Plants a trace | Offers to come down | Waits for the sanctum | Careless trace | Tidy scene | Slip of the tongue | Pushes an innocent out | Defends its own | Sacrifices its own |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Honest | 1% | 20% | 1% | 15% | 15% | 30% | 30% | 80% | 5% |
| Conscientious | 12% | 30% | 10% | 10% | 30% | 18% | 40% | 60% | 15% |
| A liar | 30% | 45% | 25% | 6% | 35% | 10% | 60% | 40% | 35% |
| Calculating | 38% | 55% | 40% | 3% | 50% | 5% | 45% | 25% | 55% |
| Detached | 15% | 20% | 20% | 8% | 35% | 12% | 30% | 30% | 20% |
| Light-headed | 10% | 40% | 5% | 30% | 5% | 35% | 60% | 70% | 10% |
| Suspicious | 22% | 25% | 15% | 10% | 30% | 15% | 55% | 50% | 25% |

- **A planted trace**: instead of its own, the attacker lays the trace of an innocent it dislikes (never the night's victim), the more readily against one who lives at the scenes (How the cult reads you, below). Unexamined it looks like any trace; examined, it shows for what it is, with the attacker's own beneath it half the time.
- **A sworn watch.** One of the cult who claims the watch swears to watch your tent as anyone who claims it would (confided in, or staging a coming clean to you alone, at trust 2 or more), for that night only. That night the cult is torn between its cover and what it knows of you (your confidences, an enemy among it), and acts on what it knows only half as readily: no more and no less than it takes for a broken watch to say about as much of anyone who swore it. If nothing came for you, the ending counts the promise kept.
- **Courting you**: a warm face without the warmth, and the same indignation as anyone when pressed for nothing; no readier trust than anyone's, except toward an occultist, whose interests it may half share (small talk warms it 8% more readily, it takes your confidence 5% more readily).
- **Careless**: the attacker leaves a second trace of its own. **Tidy**: it leaves nothing else; examined, its trace shows genuine (unless the scene misleads you) and gives up no second habit.
- **A slip of the tongue**: asked about the night (half as often in small talk), the one who did it lets slip something nobody has told the camp (when it happened, how the body lay, where they came in), once a night. An innocent who found the body or was there may let slip the same, at 35% of their temperament's rate, and it sounds the same.
- **Below**, it offers to come down with you before you ask, less readily (× 0.15) when you hardly trust it, seldom (× 0.2) once two of the camp suspect it in earnest; a patient one lets the fourth gate pass and betrays you at the very end.
- **At an inquiry** it raises its hands as anyone in its place would, from the suspicion it lets show, with only a little of its own interest on top: pushing for an innocent to go, defending its own, or giving up one of its own who looks lost (The inquiry, below).
- **Under the bus**: once its partner looks lost, one of the cult may name it before anyone else does (10% × its sacrifice rate): in a false dream, asked whom it distrusts, or blaming aloud at breakfast.
- **One of its own**, killed by the other (The night, above).
- **A frame by night**: it may kill one who has named an innocent guilty before everyone, so that with them dead the camp looks at the one they named (The night, above).
- **What it never does**: confess to being the cult. Everything else it says can be true or false.

## How the cult reads you

The cult watches how you hunt it, and turns its craft against whatever you lean on. For six of the ending's styles it counts as much of that style's own count as it can see for itself as the season goes (not what comes only below, nor your casebook's marks, nor the times you thought better of something), and sets it against an ordinary season's pace of that same count by that day. Its reading runs from nothing, at an ordinary pace or slower, to the whole of it at two spreads past; and it works against that way the more readily, by up to 50%, as far as it reads you that day.

| You lean on | It counts | It turns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The scenes (the detective) | scenes examined, nights asked about, sightings heard, the diary asked about | more planted traces and more ground swept |
| Inquiries (the inquisitor) | pressing, accusing (backed or refused), asking whom they distrust, those turned against you | pushes harder at an inquiry for an innocent to go, and comes the quicker for you once you have made an enemy of it |
| Your friends (the confidant) | confiding, asking about them, the trust and the friends you have, pretences dropped before the camp | takes those who trust you first |
| The tablets (the scholar) | translating, readings and glosses, tablet lines known | comes for you for no reason you will ever know: a lamp burning late |
| The ground (the digger) | searching, charms found | the same (the greater of the two turns): someone poking at its hiding places |
| Sitting still (the quiet guest) | resting, and never having accused anyone | grows bold: lies low less, and the rite grows faster, up to 1.3 × |

Nothing tells you. The ending says from which day it had your measure: the day its reading of a way first came to half, a spread past an ordinary pace.

## The inquiry

Nobody is sent away on your word alone: of an evening in camp, once a day, you lay out a case against one person and everyone else still at the dig raises a hand with you, speaks against it, or says nothing; the hands are shown by name and kept in the casebook.

**The case** you can make (at most 3; never shown to you): +1 for each trace at a scene that fits a habit of theirs you have seen (1.5 if examined and genuine; a thief's or a planted trace counts nothing; one found by your tent pole counts 0.5, as harm meant you rather than the night's work); +1.5 for each slip, slip of the tongue about the night, or witness against them; ±0.75 for each dream of their guilt or innocence; +1 for a sworn watch slept through; +0.5 for each flinch at what you showed them or hint at their nights; +1 for each sighting in the dark that fits a habit of theirs you have seen; +1 for each account of a night that the person they named does not bear out (one who will not say where they were bears out nothing, and belies nothing either); +0.5 for each account that puts them, by their own word, where it happened; +0.5 for being caught at your tent meaning you harm; +0.25 for each distrust voiced. The camp no longer weighs the word of one of the cult unmasked, nor the dreams of one who owned they never dreamed.

**Each hand, the cult's among them,** weighs: their own suspicion of the accused (0 to 3; the thief who saw them at it, 3) + the case + 0.5 × their trust in you + their word (below) − 0.75 × their liking for the accused + 0.15 × the night's dead so far (at most 4) − 0.25 × the camp's anger − 0.5 for each inquiry of the last two evenings that the camp refused or that sent an innocent away ± up to 1 for the evening's mood (the same for everyone; × the season's swing) ± up to 0.75 of their own whim. At 1.8 or more they side with you; below 0.5 they speak against; between, they say nothing. 2% of hands go the other way from their owner's leaning. The cult's hands add a little of its own interest (below).

**Their word.** At first most give a newcomer the benefit of the doubt: 0.5, + their temperament's (light-headed +0.4, conscientious +0.2, honest and liar 0, calculating −0.2, detached −0.3, suspicious −0.5), ± up to 0.4 for who they are. Then, each outside the cult by chance: once one of the cult is unmasked, +0.5 at 50% (+25% if they stood with you); once an innocent is sent away, −0.75 at 45% (+30% if they liked the one sent away, −20% if they stood with you, −30% × how strong your case was, out of 3); once they refused an inquiry, −0.25 at 20%; always between −1.5 and 2.5.

**The cult's hands.** The cult raises its hands as anyone in its place would, from the suspicion it lets show, so that its hands, counted over a season, look like everyone's. Its own interest leans on them only a little, by its craft: against an innocent, +0.6 × its push rate, the more once it has read you as one who leans on inquiries (How the cult reads you, above); for one of its own, −0.45 × its defend rate. But one of its own who looks lost (a case of 1.25 or more, as it looks to you, or two of the camp suspecting them in earnest) it may give up outright, at 0.5 × its sacrifice rate, and then leans 2.5 toward you. So a real case may split the cult's own hands.

**The verdict** passes with at least 2 hands with you (all there are, if fewer are left) and more with you than against; with nobody left at the dig to raise a hand, the police corporal from Hillah takes your word, or not (80%). Every hand raised is said before everyone and settled when the truth comes out.

- **Passed, and one of the cult**: unmasked, with a cylinder seal in their kit; the rite falls back about 2; those who stood with you may trust you more (60% each); the unmasked one may shout a line of the old liturgy from the truck (70%: a line that narrows the power below, 90%, else any line). Unmasking the last of the cult wins the season, all but always: 3% of the time (9% once the rite has reached 8) it has come too far to die with its priests, the gate opens of itself, and you go down that evening to an empty circle.
- **Passed, and an innocent**: every answer is true, and they leave in disgrace; the rite gains about 2; you lose about 2 nerve (not for the thief: a thief caught is a thief caught, though it counts as a wrong accusation in all else; sent away he may name the one he saw, 80%). They hold it against you (below).
- **Refused**: the accused stays and holds it against you; one of the cult learns you are close (90%); you lose about a nerve.
- **Held against you**: for good as a rule (90%: trust −2), else as one turned against you (−1); 5% of the time they half understand and it costs only a step (10% for the honest, the conscientious, or a friend).

**The camp's anger** rises about 2.5 for each innocent sent away and about 1.5 for each refused inquiry, and falls about 1 for each of the cult unmasked. After a day without an inquiry it cools by 0.5 overnight, but not below 2.5 for each innocent sent away, except on a night it lets go of that too (5%). The inquiry tells you when the camp is restless, nearly out of patience, or tired of inquiries that came to nothing. After an inquiry that went against you, from anger 3, the camp walks out at 20% for each step of anger from 3 up (never above 95%): no more inquiries, and the next day the diggers strike (85%; else Hamid, or the headman, talks them round), unless it is the last day. Each dawn after, a camp that walked out may hear you again (10%; 25% the morning after a death).

**How often an inquiry passes.** In the build-26 simulations a season saw about 0.28 inquiries, and about three in ten passed (30%), as often against one of the cult (29.5%) as against anyone else (30.5%); the camp refused the rest.

## The powers below

Each season deals four powers of eight, one of them behind the cult, and the whole puzzle anew; the omens, the tablets and the charms together pin down which power it is and which charm binds it, but no single clue does.

- **The pantheon**: Nergal, Ereshkigal, Tiamat, Lamashtu, Namtar, Pazuzu, Kingu, Belet-Seri. Four are in play each season, one behind the cult.
- **Signs**: forty-eight, eight of each of six natures (fire, dust, the salt sea, teeth, plague, madness); eight are in play. Each sign belongs to exactly two of the season's powers and each power has four, so no single sign names a power. A power takes the nature of the first of its signs: its colour in the deaths, the nights and the sanctum, what it favours between the gates, and the doom that ends the season if the rite completes.
- **Titles**: thirty-two old titles, two to each power, dealt anew every season, so knowing the hymns tells you nothing.
- **Charms**: twelve (a lapis cylinder seal, an obsidian eye-stone, an inscribed clay cone, two clay figures, a bronze demon head, a shell-inlaid plaque, a clay liver model, a silver crescent, a bronze bell, a haematite duck-weight, an etched carnelian bead, an agate fly). Six lie in the tell, one to a place as a rule (10% of seasons two share a place and another holds none): four are wards, one per power, two bind nothing. Each charm bears two marks of fifteen (seven notches, a hair-crack, a smell of cedar, a fleck of gold…): one no other charm bears, and a second that another charm may bear too (two marks are shared so). The tablets never name a charm, only its marks, by its own mark alone or by both (50% of seasons, always the same way in a season); you learn a charm's marks when you come by it (1% of the time one is misread).
- **Glosses** tie titles to powers: Anselm, Ilse and Zahrun gloss a title you ask about (Conversations, above); a philologist starts knowing one.

**Every title is a real one.** All thirty-two old titles are attested titles or names of Mesopotamian powers, as the old texts give them (Nergal's Meslamtaea, "the One Who Came Out of Meslam"; Neti's "the Chief Doorman of the Underworld", from Inana's Descent; Tiamat's "Mother Hubur", from Enuma elish; the demon rabisu, "the Lurker"), and so is each power's own epithet (Tiamat, "She Who Bore Them All"; Namtar, "Vizier of the Underworld"; Belet-Seri, "Scribe of the Underworld"). Dealt anew each season to any of the powers, they still tell you nothing on their own.

**The tablets** hold every line the fragments can give, shuffled; one line in a hundred is a scribe's error, false in one thing, and never told as such.

| Line | What it says | How it is dealt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signs | Under a sign: the two powers who wake ("If …: A or B is waking"), the two who do not, or one line for each | One line for both, 50% (naming the two who wake 50%, else the two who do not); else a line each, naming one who wakes 50%, else one who never stirs |
| Names | "X is one of the names of N", or "X and Y are one power under two titles" | Every power gets both its titles named 40% of the time, else one named and the other tied to it |
| Apart | "X and Y are not one power" | Each pair of titles of different powers, 30%, at most two such lines |
| Wards | What bears a mark is tied to a sign of its power; then to a second sign of a different partner, to a pair of powers, or to its power by name or title | Second sign 40%, a pair 35%, its power 25% |
| Not wards | "What bears … has no hold over X" | One for every ward, naming another power |
| A pair | Two charms bound together, one of them a toy | 60% of seasons |
| Toys | "What bears … binds no power at all" | One for each of the two decoys |

A power is named by an old title rather than its name 80% of the time. A fragment's lines lean toward what you are studying (60%), and a line asked for about the power below narrows it 90% of the time. The lines together, a scribe's error apart, always pin down the power and its ward; no line alone does.

## The descent

On the fifth night, or before dawn on the night the rite reaches 12, a stair opens under the ziggurat and you go down. (If the last of the cult is unmasked above ground and the rite has already gone too far to die with its priests, 3% of the time, 9% once the rite stands at 8 or more, the stair opens there and then with nobody left to finish the rite.) You name a power, choose who comes with you, and pass seven gates: the stair at the first, one or two trials between it and the fourth, the fourth gate itself, one or two more trials, and the seventh gate, which asks a price. Beyond it is the sanctum, where the rite is finished or stopped. No choice down here shows its odds, only what it risks. Nerve at nothing below is *Two Hundred and Nine Steps*; wounds at the limit, *Buried with the Kings*.

**Naming a power.** Before you go down you name one of the season's four powers, or none and go down blind. The right name adds 8% to the stair, to the eyes at the fourth gate and to every trial, adds 15% to the true ward, and lets you speak the counter-rite. A wrong name gives none of that, and the counter-rite against it works 4% of the time.

### Who comes with you

Up to two. Before you ask, some offer (two at most, drawn at random from those who would):

| Who | Offers to come |
| --- | --- |
| Anyone turned against you (trust below 0) | 1% |
| Anyone else, at trust 0, 1, 2 or more | 5%, 25%, 50% |
| One of the cult | Honest 20%, conscientious 30%, liar 45%, calculating 55%, detached 20%, light-headed 40%, suspicious 25%; × 0.15 at trust 0, × 0.2 if two or more of the camp suspect it |

Asked, one who offered comes 97% of the time. Anyone else (and an offer that falls through) comes unless:

| Who | Comes |
| --- | --- |
| Trust −2 | 3%, out of duty |
| Trust −1 | 35% if honest or conscientious (out of duty), else 3% |
| Cannot bear someone already coming (likes them −2) | 25%, through gritted teeth |
| Otherwise | All but the afraid: 12% are too afraid at trust 1 or less, 3% of friends (trust 2 or more) |

The cult answers as anyone would in its place. At the top of the stair your nerve steadies by about 1 for each companion, whatever they are. Below, a loyal companion (one not of the cult, still with you) adds 10% to the stair and the fourth gate, and in the sanctum 10% to the true ward and the counter-rite and 4% to the fire. One of the cult with you adds nothing, and acts for the cult.

### The sanctum

**Who stands in the circle.** Everyone of the cult still free stands in the circle, except in three cases:

- **Exposed or taken.** One exposed at an inquiry or taken down at the fourth gate does not stand there.
- **Came down with you.** One of the cult who came down with you (or held the seventh gate) takes its place at the head of the circle or beside it 95% of the time, 66.5% if fond of you (trust 2 or more). Otherwise it stops at the edge of the light, looks at you, and does not go to them.
- **Left behind on the way.** One left behind comes by another way 95% of the time, and otherwise stays away.

When one of the cult with you takes its place and you carry the true ward, 35% of the time it took the ward from you on the way, and burns it before your eyes.

**An empty circle.** With nobody of the cult in it (the last of it unmasked too late, killed by your own hand, or stopped at the edge of the light), the rite has no voice to finish it: *The Empty Sanctum*, a clean victory, 97% of the time. Otherwise the power finishes the rite itself, and the ending tells it so.

**The acts.** Each act is one throw:

| Act | Odds |
| --- | --- |
| Raise the true ward | 55% + the bonus + 15% if you named the power below + 10% for an occultist |
| Raise any other charm | 3% |
| The counter-rite (only with a power named) | Against the power below: 25% + the bonus + 4% for each tablet line you know about it (up to 5) + 15% for a philologist + 15% for an occultist. Against the wrong one: 4% |
| Shoot (with the revolver): the hierophant, or the first of the circle | 25% with one of the cult in the circle, 6% with more |
| Overturn the braziers into the bitumen (once) | 6% + 4% for each loyal companion − 0.5% for each step of the rite |

The bonus is:

- +10% for each loyal companion still with you;
- +2% for each point of nerve above 5, or −2% for each point below;
- −3% for each step of the rite;
- −7% for each of the cult in the circle beyond the first;
- +6% for each point of favour;
- +8% once an act has failed (the circle was shaken).

Odds are kept within 5% and 95%, the two flat odds (3% and 4%) apart.

**When an act fails**, a ward or the counter-rite costs about 1 nerve. A shot costs about a nerve and a wound, and the revolver is taken from you. The fire also costs about a nerve and a wound. Any of these can end you there. After the first failure there is time for another act 97% of the time, after the second 5%, and after that 1%; otherwise the rite completes.

**Nothing left to try** (no charm, no name, no revolver, the fire spent): you can only watch. 3% of the time the rite breaks of itself on its last line (*What the Gate Kept*); otherwise it completes.

**Clean or hard.** A ward or the counter-rite that works at the first act, with nerve at 6 or more and a wound to spare, is a clean victory 90% of the time and a hard one otherwise. A later act, or one without nerve or a wound to spare, is hard, and clean 3% of the time. A shot or the fire is always a hard victory.

A ward, the rite or a fire that wins is told for what it was: a charm against another power, or the wrong name, that worked all the same is told as the luck it was. A clean win by the dice's 3% grace (above) is still a clean win.

**The conduct score.** +3 for each of the cult exposed at an inquiry; +2 for each taken down at the fourth gate or unmasked in the night (one who came to your tent and failed to kill you); +2 if the hierophant was exposed; −3 for each wrong accusation; −1 for each inquiry the camp refused; −2 if the camp ever walked out; −2 for each of the night's dead (not counting whoever you killed yourself, whom the deed counts); +1 for each right mark and −1 for each wrong one in your casebook, as it stood when that person's part came out (a mark changed after the fact does not count); +2 for naming the power rightly below (−1 wrongly); +2 for a first exposure by day 3; −1 for each failed act below; up to ±2 for the camp's trust at the end (friends at 2 or more, less those you turned against you); +1 if anyone came clean before the camp; for the deed, −1 for going out at all and, if you killed, +2 for one of the cult or −5 for anyone else; −1 for each wound you carry; +1 if your nerve ends at 70% of its most or more; −1 if it ever fell to 2. A win is clean at 10 or more, honest at 5, costly at 0, otherwise at any price (a win with blood on your hands is a costly one at best); a defeat was within reach at 4 or more, otherwise you were out of your depth.

**Your style** is what you did unusually much of, against an ordinary season's mean and spread: the inquisitor (pressing and accusing × 2, distrust asked × 0.5, enemies made × 0.5, facing a traitor × 2, a shot × 2), the quiet guest (resting × 2, backing off × 2, the slow stair, going unseen, never accusing × 2), the scholar (translating × 2, readings × 1.5, lines known × 0.3, the counter-rite × 2), the detective (examining × 2, asking about nights, sightings and the diary × 0.5 each, marks right × 0.5), the digger (searching × 2, charms × 0.5), the confidant (confiding × 2, small talk, trust, friends, confessions heard, companions below). The style that stands out most by at least 0.75 of a spread names you; with nothing out of the ordinary, you were the field director. A season in which you killed someone with your own hands is the Avenger's, whatever else you did: eight styles in all. In play: field director 19.5%, digger 14.8%, scholar 13.5%, inquisitor 13.4%, confidant 13.4%, quiet guest 12.2%, detective 11.4%, the Avenger 1.8%.

**How much of Kutha came home with you** (a won season's account): horrors met (1 each), nerve lost to reading (1 each), every 5 lines known, wounds taken in the night (1 each), every 2 signs seen, every 3 charms found, a memory left at the seventh gate (2), being an occultist (1), and each step your nerve ever fell below 3 (1 each), added up: at 7.5 a little, at 10.5 a great deal.

**The account remarks on** a first exposure early or late; four or more friends at the end (most of the camp would have followed you) or three or more enemies; three or more marks right; nerve at the end in rags (3 or less, never having fallen to 2).

**The debrief** tells the truth of the season: every part, temperament, claim and feeling toward you; the power, its signs, titles and ward and the marks by which the tablets knew it; why the night chose each of its dead, and why each quiet night was quiet; the cult's planted traces and kept promises, and from which day it had your measure; the true Dreamer's muddied or dark dreams, and your own dreams and whether each was so; the night someone came to your tent and the night you went out yourself; every rare turn of the dice (a scribe's error, a stray sign, a misnoted habit, a death in another manner, a bowl that broke and held nothing, a misread mark…); and what became of everyone, with a line or two more drawn from what passed between you (none 30%, one 45%, two 25%; your own, one 60%, two 40%). The epilogues are fixed by the season without the dice, so the same season is told the same way twice.

**The end you chose.** Rarely, what the season left in you is more than you can carry, and in the end you end it yourself; the epilogue never says how, only that it was and something of why. Only if you came out of the season alive: 1.4% for anyone who came home, × 5 if your mind broke and the season left you in a quiet ward, else × 2 if your nerve all but broke; × 1.5 in a cell in Baghdad for the one you killed; × 3 for one you killed who was no cultist, or × 1.5 for one who was; × 1.4 for each innocent sent away on your word; × 1.3 for each friend the night took; × 1.6 if someone died beside you; × 1.5 if the reading cost you dear; × 1.4 if the horrors stayed with you; × 0.8 for each friend who came through, up to three; × 0.4 if someone loves you; at most 25%. It is told by one of its reasons, the weightier likelier, and told last of all; your other epilogue is then one line at most, and never the same thing. Measured on the bots: about 3½% of seasons, nearly all of them a mind that broke (about 8% of the madness endings), almost never a season won.

**A death that came later.** Now and then one of the seven who came through the season does not come through what it left in them: by their own hand, or in a way nobody can account for. For each who is still at the dig, sent away, or of the cult in a cell: 4.5%, × 1.3 if you did not come home, × 3 for one sent away in disgrace (not the thief), × 2 for the true Dreamer, × 1.6 for one who grieved, × 1.6 for one who went down with you, × 2.5 for one of the cult in a cell, × 1.5 for the thief; at most 30% each, and at most two in an ending, the likeliest. It is told by one of its reasons, the weightier likelier, and is by their own hand 40% to 70% of the time as that reason has it (never for one who went below, or the thief; never a priest, who keeps his vows); it is all that is told of them. Never one spoken for in a romance, never one of the cult still at large, never after a season the gate opened. Measured over 2,100 seasons: one in about 18% of seasons (two in 1–2%), a third of them by their own hand.

## How often each ending comes

Played as a person would, a season is lost about 90% of the time, won hard about 7% and won clean about 2%; style, mood, the power and your background move these by a few points at most.

These are 3,150 simulated seasons of build 26.1.0: bots that play the real page, each leaning toward one way of working on top of the same sensible play, 450 seasons each, every background and season's mood as the dice dealt them. A person who reads the casebook well should do better than these bots, a careless one worse. Intervals are 95% (Wilson); a row of 450 seasons is good to about ±3 points.
