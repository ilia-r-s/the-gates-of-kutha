# Manipulation techniques, applied to Kutha

Oct 27, 2026 · @Ilia

The hidden cult in The Gates of Kutha can counter every way of hunting it without a statistical tell if it works through the camp's innocents and never acts where the player looks. Nearly every covert method on record keeps its author one step back, so that onlookers see only sincere people repeating, doubting, cooling and voting. That holds for the Stasi's *Zersetzung*, for Soviet active measures, for the FBI's "snitch-jacket", for the indirect aggression and mobbing of ordinary workplaces, and for the hidden side's best play in Werewolf, Mafia and The Traitors. For Kutha this means the cult's own visible behaviour should come from the same rates as everyone's: how often it starts rumours, accuses, defends, votes and speaks. Its influence should travel only as small shifts in what innocents believe and pass on. The record is just as clear about where such campaigns leak, and it is not their voice but their aim and their coordination. On Japanese Werewolf boards, the first ordinary villager the wolves attacked had named a wolf as top suspect in **8 of 14 cases, a likelihood ratio of about 5.3**. In Avalon, a bot that simply hunted the most accurate voter cut an informed player's win rate **from 43.1% to 4.2%**. Telling the hidden side by its words alone sits barely above chance: **AUROC 0.57 to 0.68 for classifiers, 54% for human lie detection**. Outside games, the covert accounts a South Korean intelligence service ran during the 2012 presidential election were told apart by **traces of coordination among the agents, not by any one account's characteristics**. Real operations were usually exposed in a single break: a seized file, a defector, or one act too visible.

Eight lessons follow, each building on the earlier report's finding that the cult is caught by what it knows:

1. Seed once and let an innocent carry it.
2. Slant a true fact rather than invent a false one.
3. Imply rather than assert, because visible intent provokes resistance and stains whoever speaks.
4. Attack each style's source of evidence, and let the player's style trigger it, never the truth of the evidence.
5. Dilute the aim with deliberate misses, and give every cult effect an innocent cause at a similar rate.
6. Make the inquisitor's backlash a property of the whole camp that the cult only nudges. Hard questioning produces false signs of guilt and resentment unaided.
7. Keep the one open act to a single planted item or a disguised accident, the way the record's most lasting frame-ups worked.
8. Keep the pair and the clock ordinary. The two cultists' joint behaviour, the timing of their whispers around camp events and the steadiness of their results must look like anyone's, not only each cultist's own rates.

Of the 34 ideas weighed below, **13 apply, 14 need adapting, 3 are already in the game, 2 wait for later and 2 are rejected**. 28.3.0 built three of the lessons (What 28.3.0 built from this, below). The notes on organised tradecraft remain partial on cults and on how they kept their discretion. A fourth note file covers how covert schemes and confidence tricks were detected and exposed, at the level of documented history and analysis.

## Covert influence works by moving the people around the target

Rumour grows where a matter is both important and ambiguous. That is the "basic law" of Allport and Postman, as summarised in a 2022 review letter ([Naji 2022](https://ijps.tums.ac.ir/index.php/ijps/article/download/2992/1071)). A field study of two real rumour episodes, a student's death from meningitis and the Washington sniper shootings, found that **belief is the main driver of passing a rumour on, and anxiety mainly amplifies it**: belief mattered most for high-anxiety rumours ([Pezzo & Beckstead 2006](https://pure.lib.usf.edu/en/publications/a-multilevel-analysis-of-rumor-transmission-effects-of-anxiety-an-2/)). This conflicts with older theory that treated anxiety itself as a main cause. A dig camp grieving an unexplained death is close to an ideal setting for rumour.

Ordinary gossip is common but mostly harmless. In 467 people wearing recorders, talk about absent others filled about **52 minutes a day, roughly 14% of conversation**. Of 4,003 gossip instances, **about 15% were negative and 75% neutral** ([UC Riverside release on Robbins & Karan](https://webarchive.ucr.edu/news.ucr.edu/articles/2019/05/03/uc-riverside-study-busts-myths-about-gossip.html)). The notes multiply these into a baseline: about **2% of all talk is negative talk about someone absent**. A camp at a murder will run hotter than that. What matters for Kutha is that both sides run at the same rate.

Who is believed decides which route a covert whisper should take. Gossip seemed truer when it came from several sources, and truer still when they were independent. It seemed less true when a benign explanation was offered or when the gossiper competed with the target ([Hess & Hagen 2006](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s12110-006-1013-z)). A single voice can still fake a crowd. When one group member stated an opinion three times, listeners' estimate of how widely it was held rose from **4.66 to 5.43, against 6.22 when three people said it once each (d = 0.74)**. The effect held among the 137 of 177 participants who knew there was one source. It held even when 271 of 305 noticed the statements were identical photocopies (44.18% vs 38.26%) ([Weaver et al. 2007](https://dornsife.usc.edu/norbert-schwarz/wp-content/uploads/sites/231/2023/11/07_jpsp_weaver_et_al_chorus.pdf)). Prior knowledge blocked the effect at first, but the block wore off after a delay (4.37 vs 3.55).

Two cautions apply to these findings. The authors' headline that repetition achieves "90%" of the three-voice effect is not a stable number: ratios computed from the reported means range from about 33% to over 100%. And Weaver's consensus measure cannot be compared directly with Hess and Hagen's truth measure. Across 51 studies, repetition also raises perceived truth ([Dechêne et al. 2010](https://edoc.unibas.ch/10139)).

Open accusation has a built-in cost, and rumours fade unevenly. Listeners unconsciously attribute to a speaker the traits the speaker describes in others. This **spontaneous trait transference** persisted even when people were told that speakers and statements had been paired at random ([ScienceDaily release on Skowronski et al. 1998](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/1998/03/980319071139.htm)).

Bad news about warmth and morality is easy to confirm and hard to undo, while bad news about competence is not ([Tausch et al. 2007](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/the-confirmability-and-disconfirmability-of-trait-concepts-revisi/)). A liked person can lose credibility "after a few unlikable acts" ([Vonk 2001](https://cdn2.psychologytoday.com/assets/aversive-behaving_badly_2001.pdf)).

Do people forget where a claim came from and keep the claim? The evidence is narrower than folklore suggests. Across 72 data sets, a true "sleeper effect" appeared only when the discrediting cue came after the message and the audience was engaged (**d = 0.25 against a baseline of 0.08**). Source memory was measured in only 22% of those data sets ([Kumkale & Albarracín 2004](https://www.asc.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/2021-07/e3eff4_67a5be5947dc44208b8668beb6ceadd6.pdf)).

The quietest forms of influence keep intent off the record:

- **Faint praise.** Praising someone only for warmth leads listeners to infer weak competence, and it lowered their willingness to include the person in their group ([Kervyn, Bergsieker & Fiske 2012](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/the-innuendo-effect-hearing-the-positive-but-inferring-the-negati/)).
- **Veiled hints.** Indirect speech preserves deniability. In the formal model it pays only when different listeners need different amounts of proof before they act ([Pinker, Nowak & Lee 2008](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/pinker/files/pnas_logic_of_indirect_speech.pdf)).
- **Questions.** The evidence on "just asking questions" is thin. A conference poster found that question headlines did not raise belief in their content ([Harris & Calvillo 2022](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/conventions/archive/2022annual/paper/39843/)), and the classic study of innuendo by question could not be verified ([Wegner et al. 1981, listed](https://dtg.sites.fas.harvard.edu/DANWEGNER/wjh/socog.htm)).
- **When intent shows.** Listeners resist when they can see a persuasive aim. A meta-analysis of 20 studies (4,942 participants) found this resistance, called reactance, to be anger and counter-arguing combined ([Rains 2013](https://academic.oup.com/hcr/article-abstract/39/1/47/4093682)).
- **Paltering**, misleading with true statements, is admitted by more than half of executives in one course. Once exposed, it is judged as harshly as lying ([APA release on Rogers et al. 2016](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2016/12/true-lies)).

Indirect aggression is defined by acting "on the people around the attacked person" rather than confronting them. It is the most common adult form of aggression and draws less social disapproval than direct aggression ([Anguiano-Carrasco & Vigil-Colet 2011](https://www.psicothema.com/pdf/3863.pdf)).

Workplace mobbing shows how such campaigns usually run:

- **Prevalence.** In Leymann's Swedish sample, **3.5% (±0.7%) of workers were being mobbed at any time**.
- **Who drives it.** About **a third of cases were driven by one person and just over 40% by two to four**. Whole groups turning on someone was "very rare."
- **How it ends.** Management "tends to accept and assume the prejudices" of earlier stages. Colleagues explain the conflict by the victim's own character ([Leymann 1996](https://www.kwesthues.com/e-LeymannEJWOP1996.pdf)).

How visible a tactic is follows how open it is. In 59 dating couples, coercion was the most observable tactic in the text's account, although the table conflicts, with reason at .77. Partners agreed on each other's tactics only **.42 for starting a behaviour and .25 for stopping one** ([Buss et al. 1987](https://cdn.la.utexas.edu/buss/files/2015/09/tactics_of_manipulation_1987_jpsp.pdf)). People high in "dark triad" traits vary their tactics, which the authors read as a way to avoid detection ([Jonason & Webster 2012](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/a-protean-approach-to-social-influence-dark-triad-personalities-a/)).

Groups look for a culprit more eagerly when they feel guilty about a bad outcome or when it has no explanation ([Rothschild et al. 2012](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/a-dual-motive-model-of-scapegoating-displacing-blame-to-reduce-gu/)). Bystanders who judge a mistreated person **undeserving turn angry at the aggressor and support the target. Those who judge the target deserving join in excluding them** ([Mitchell, Vogel & Folger 2015](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/scopus2015/202)).

Two gaps run through this section. Björkqvist's "effect/danger ratio" could not be checked against its source. And **no study gives a rate at which people in natural groups identify a rumour's originator or the instigator of indirect aggression**. The only evidence is indirect: memory of a claim's source fades faster than the claim.

## Secret police, cults and swindlers hid the author until one break exposed them

### Zersetzung

The Stasi's 1976 directive made *Zersetzung* ("decomposition") a written, centrally approved alternative to arrest, used where an arrest was not politically convenient. Against individuals it prescribed the "systematic discrediting of public reputation" and the "systematic organisation of professional and social failures to undermine self-confidence." The discrediting was to rest on **interlocking true, verifiable facts combined with untrue ones** ([Richtlinie 1/76, Bl. 353](https://www.stasi-mediathek.de/embed/richtlinie-176-zur-bearbeitung-operativer-vorgaenge/blatt/353/embed/1/); [Bl. 355](https://www.stasi-mediathek.de/embed/richtlinie-176-zur-bearbeitung-operativer-vorgaenge/blatt/355/embed/1/)).

Against groups it prescribed creating "mistrust and mutual suspicion" and exploiting rivalries. Its tested means included anonymous letters and calls, and "feigning that MfS counter-measures had been exposed." Its main instruments were trusted informers (IMs) working under cover stories, among them "confidants of the group's leader" ([Bl. 355](https://www.stasi-mediathek.de/embed/richtlinie-176-zur-bearbeitung-operativer-vorgaenge/blatt/355/embed/1/)).

In practice the attack followed a set order:

1. The private sphere came first, with objects quietly moved inside homes.
2. Rumours of affairs followed.
3. Then came engineered failures at work ([bpb, Trobisch-Lütge 2016](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-teilung/stasi/218417/psychofolgen-bis-heute-zersetzungs-opfer-der-ddr-geheimpolizei/)).

Informers stalled group decisions with "constant counter-proposals." The Stasi also made members look like informers and granted some of them privileges so that others would suspect them. Historians estimate **4,500 to 5,000 operational cases a year in 1985–88**. Victims were "almost invariably not believed" and were often written off as paranoid. Systematic exposure came only when the files opened after 1990 ([Wikipedia: Zersetzung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zersetzung)).

### Soviet active measures

Soviet active measures followed the same principle at scale. A disinformation message had to "at least partially correspond to reality." It was planted where its origin was invisible, then cited later as if it came from an independent source, and carried by sincere believers who did not know where it had started. Moscow treated repetition over time as the key to success ([Boghardt 2009](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/1-Operation_INFEKTION_Soviet_Bloc_AIDS_Disinformation_Campaign.pdf)).

The AIDS campaign shows the sequence:

- **17 July 1983**: an anonymous letter in an Indian paper. Nobody picked it up.
- **October 1985**: a Soviet weekly revived the claim, citing that letter as its source.
- **1986**: a retired biologist, a sincere believer supplied with material by East German intelligence, gave the claim scientific credibility.
- **Late 1987**: the story had appeared in media in **80 countries**.

American analysts traced the chain of citations backward but missed the 1983 letter, because their search began only in 1984. Proof of Soviet authorship came only from insiders years later ([Boghardt 2009](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/1-Operation_INFEKTION_Soviet_Bloc_AIDS_Disinformation_Campaign.pdf)).

### COINTELPRO and the snitch-jacket

The FBI's COINTELPRO (1956–71) aimed to "expose, disrupt, misdirect, discredit, or otherwise neutralize" its targets ([archived COINTELPRO page](https://www.faculty.umb.edu/gary_zabel/Courses/Morals%20and%20Law/M+L/August,%2005/COINTELPRO%20-%20Wikipedia,%20the%20free%20encyclopedia.htm)). The Church Committee's list of its techniques includes "Efforts to Promote Enmity and Factionalism" and "Disseminating Derogatory Information to Family, Friends, and Associates" ([Church Committee, Book III](https://aarclibrary.org/publib/church/reports/book3/contents.htm)).

Its snitch-jacket ("bad-jacketing") manufactured evidence that a target was an informer ([Wikipedia: Bad-jacketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad-jacketing)). In July 1964 forged documents were planted where William Albertson's comrades would find them. His party of almost 40 years expelled him as a "police agent," and he never cleared his name. The truth surfaced only in 1976, through a records request that turned up an FBI report of January 1965, **twelve years later**. **One planted item was enough, and the group's own vigilance did the rest** ([Dickinson College, "Who was William Albertson?"](https://blogs.dickinson.edu/hist-fbi/?p=109)).

### Cults

High-control groups defend themselves on two fronts. Inwardly they control information:

- need-to-know rules and separate doctrines for insiders and outsiders;
- members who report on each other;
- critics labelled as bad in advance ([BITE model](https://freedomofmind.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/BITE-model.pdf));
- a "heavenly deception" that licenses lying to outsiders, with leaders tightening control as outside pressure grows ([Lifton excerpt](https://freedomofmind.com/?p=17634)).

Outwardly, Scientology's 1966 policy ordered: "NEVER agree to an investigation of Scientology. ONLY agree to an investigation of the attackers" ([Miller, *Bare-Faced Messiah*](https://religio.de/books/bfm/makeitro.html)). Its "noisy investigation" was meant to intimidate. Investigators told a target's doctor, dentist, friends and neighbours that the target was being investigated "for criminal activities" ([Wikipedia: Noisy investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noisy_investigation)).

The campaign against the writer Paulette Cooper escalated in stages:

1. Anonymous smears.
2. Bomb threats forged on her own fingerprinted stationery. She was indicted in May 1973, and the charges were dropped in 1975.
3. A 1976 plan, "Freakout", to get her jailed or committed. It included a separate channel to gather information from her to measure whether the other channels were working. The plan was never carried out.

Exposure came through overreach elsewhere. Two Guardian's Office agents, busy with a separate infiltration of federal agencies, were noticed in government buildings in 1976, and one of them later defected. An FBI raid in July 1977 then seized **more than 48,000 documents** ([Wikipedia: Operation Freakout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Freakout)).

The Rajneeshee commune's 1984 salmonella attack sickened **at least 751 people and put 45 in hospital**. Investigators considered deliberate contamination and rejected it: there was no motive, no claim of responsibility and no disgruntled employee. The attack came out only through members' testimony and a vial of the bacterium found a year later ([Török et al. 1997](https://www.cdc.gov/phlp/docs/forensic_epidemiology/Additional%20Materials/Articles/Torok%20et%20al.pdf)).

### Confidence tricks

A confidence trick keeps its secret in the mark rather than in the swindler. In Erving Goffman's classic account, the mark is allowed to win, drawn into a rigged venture and then dropped (the "blowoff"). Where trouble threatens, one operator, the "cooler", stays behind to talk him into accepting the loss quietly.

The mark stays quiet because of his self-image. He sees himself as "a pretty shrewd person when it comes to making deals," so at the blowoff he "must face the fact that he is only another easy mark," and complaining would make that public. Goffman's catalogue of cooling applies to everyday failure as well: consolation, a lesser status as a "consolation prize", stalling so that the mark has no clear target, and a face-saving exit "of his own accord". When cooling fails, the mark may "squawk" to an authority, turn sour, or go into business for himself ([Goffman 1952, reading copy](https://jthomasniu.org/class/377a/Readings/goffmark.html)).

Maria Konnikova adds that cons flourish in "revolutions, wars, crazes, crashes, rushes, and upheaval," and among people "feeling isolated, lonely, heartbroken, or otherwise emotionally vulnerable." Many victims refuse to admit the loss "even after they have all the evidence in front of them" ([Kirkus interview](https://kirkusreviews.com/news-and-features/articles/maria-konnikova)). As one review sums her up: "When we are emotionally raw or flustered, we are especially vulnerable to a scam" ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/review-the-confidence-game/)).

Survey evidence agrees. An AARP study surveyed 723 confirmed fraud victims, identified from law-enforcement lists. **Only 37% of those over 55 acknowledged being scammed when asked, against 56% of younger victims. Only 25% had reported it to an authority, against 44%** ([Senior Housing News on the AARP study](https://seniorhousingnews.com/2011/06/06/senior-fraud-cases-are-underreported-says-new-aarp-study/)).

Zersetzung's victims faced the same "is it me?" problem. Covert entries and moved belongings were meant to make them doubt their own sanity ([bpb](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-teilung/stasi/218417/psychofolgen-bis-heute-zersetzungs-opfer-der-ddr-geheimpolizei/)).

### How the authors were caught

**Exposure came in a single break, not from coincidences slowly added up.** In each documented case the break took one of three forms:

- a stolen or seized paper trail;
- an insider who defected or confessed;
- one act visible enough to draw scrutiny.

Warnings usually existed earlier but were dismissed, misattributed or not followed up. The operations ran for **about 4 to about 20 years** before exposure:

| Case | Ran from | What exposed it | Roughly how long |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| COINTELPRO | 1956 | Files stolen from an FBI field office in March 1971, then a two-year records fight over one unexplained word | 15 years |
| Zersetzung | 1976 directive | The files, after 1989; a victim's 1977 account had been dismissed | 13 years or more |
| An informer reporting on his own wife | 1972 | His role came to light before their 1992 divorce | about 20 years |
| INFEKTION | 1983 | US traced the funding of the first outlet in 1986; insiders admitted the operation in 1990–92 | 3 years to attribution, 9 to admission |
| Secondary Infektion | 2014 | Platform takedowns, 2019 | about 5 years |
| Operation Snow White | 1973 | Bystanders noticed agents in 1976; a defector came forward in 1977 | 3–4 years |
| Madoff | complaints from 1992 | His own confession, December 2008 | 16 years after the first complaint |

Sources for the table: [NYRB 2014](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2014/10/23/burglars-who-exposed-fbi/); [Wikipedia: Zersetzung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zersetzung); [Wikipedia: Vera Lengsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Lengsfeld); [Boghardt](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/1-Operation_INFEKTION_Soviet_Bloc_AIDS_Disinformation_Campaign.pdf); [AP via KOMO](https://komonews.com/news/nation-world/report-russia-linked-disinformation-operation-still-active); [Wikipedia: Operation Snow White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Snow_White); [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2009/09/sec-inspector-general-report-details/).

#### Paper trails

**The bureaucracies were convicted by their own paper trails.**

- On 8 March 1971 eight activists burgled the FBI's office in Media, Pennsylvania. Among the files was a routing slip that used the word "COINTELPRO" without explaining it. One reporter needed nearly two years, including litigation, to learn what it meant ([NYRB 2014](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2014/10/23/burglars-who-exposed-fbi/)).
- The Church Committee then reviewed **110,000 documents and heard about 800 witnesses** ([U.S. Senate](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/investigations/church-committee.htm)).
- Zersetzung required written plans approved by a department head, or by the minister when they targeted groups. That is how the archive now documents its cases ([Bundesarchiv, MfS-Lexikon](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/im-archiv-recherchieren/stasi-unterlagen-einsehen/hinweise-zum-mfs/mfs-lexikon/detail/zersetzung/)).
- The Guardian's Office routed stolen documents up its chain of command, and the 1977 raids filled a 16-ton truck ([Wikipedia: Operation Snow White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Snow_White)).

#### Insiders and visible moments

**Insiders and visible moments did the rest.**

- A cleaning woman, a guard and a night librarian noticed the same two Scientology operatives in federal buildings in 1976, before anyone understood the operation. One of the two later escaped his own group's custody and surrendered to the FBI ([Wikipedia: Operation Snow White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Snow_White)).
- In 1978 a trusted financial secretary defected from the Peoples Temple. Her affidavit helped bring Congressman Leo Ryan to Guyana ([Jonestown Institute](https://jonestown.sdsu.edu/?p=13072)).
- Madoff drew six complaints between 1992 and 2008. Regulators saw "steady returns" and an advisory business earning far more than it should, but never verified a single trade with an independent third party ([Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2009/09/03/103505.htm); [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2009/09/sec-inspector-general-report-details/)).
- INFEKTION's first letter carried the producer's fingerprints: "virus flu" for "flu virus", an obscure military journal as its source, and impossible science. Its outlet turned out to be financed by the KGB. Yet a US spokesman's mistaken denial that the letter existed handed Moscow a propaganda win ([Boghardt](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/1-Operation_INFEKTION_Soviet_Bloc_AIDS_Disinformation_Campaign.pdf)).

#### Coordination, timing and steady results

**Afterwards, investigators read coordination, timing and steadiness, not individual behaviour.**

- **Coordination.** A court case published a list of the South Korean National Intelligence Service's accounts from the 2012 election. Using it as ground truth, Keller and colleagues found the campaign accounts distinguished by "traces left by coordination among astroturfing agents, instead of the individual account characteristics" ([Keller et al., *Political Communication*](https://ideals.illinois.edu/items/112547)). Two independent cluster analyses, one of activity and one of text, recovered the same three groups of agents ([Keller et al., ICWSM 2017](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/14941)).
- **Timing.** China's "50 Cent" commentators produced about **488 million fabricated posts a year**. They wrote in bursts timed to controversies and planned protests, which made them recognisable once a leaked archive supplied a starting set ([NPR via TPR](https://www.tpr.org/science-technology/2016-05-22/study-chinas-government-fabricates-about-488-million-social-media-posts-every-year)).
- **Steadiness.** Madoff's tell was returns too steady to be natural.

In each case the statistical pattern became evidence only after a leak or a court case had supplied ground truth to check it against.

#### Concealment against impact

**Concealment and impact pulled against each other.** "Secondary Infektion" stayed hidden for about five years by posting through throwaway accounts and putting "a lot of emphasis on staying hidden, rather than quick virality." "Almost none of those efforts achieved measurable impact" ([AP via KOMO](https://komonews.com/news/nation-world/report-russia-linked-disinformation-operation-still-active)). The Korean accounts likewise "only had a limited impact" ([Keller et al.](https://ideals.illinois.edu/items/112547)).

#### A planted "who was spared?"

**The "who was spared?" heuristic could be planted.** In the Stasi's operation "Pegasus" (1976), several people were arrested. The Stasi then spread suspicion that one man, never arrested or questioned, was the informer. Some of the deported detainees repeated it in public, and the rumour was meant to dissolve only once the files were opened ([LAkD Brandenburg 2024](https://zeitzeugen.brandenburg.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Zersetzung_Textbeitrag-der-LAkD-2024-mit-Literatur.pdf)). The same source notes that harm through official channels, such as a lost job or an adverse decision, was relatively easy to recognise as repression. Harm in private life was much harder to identify.

**No author in these cases was caught by demeanour.**

## In deduction games the hidden side is caught by its aim, not its voice

### Six discreet methods

The game notes sort the hidden side's discreet methods into six families:

1. Disguising votes.
2. Seeding contradictions.
3. Amplifying an innocent's mistaken suspicion.
4. Discrediting the source of information.
5. Controlling the procedure.
6. Silencing the accurate accuser.

### Votes between partners

How often partners vote against each other depends on who is playing:

- **The Traitors**: **95% of 76 votes cast against traitors came from faithful players** ([Kramer, via The Conversation](https://www.nationaltribune.com.au/celebrity-traitors-my-research-shows-voting-behaviour-could-help-identify-faithfuls/)).
- **One Night Werewolf** (lab study): werewolves voted for a fellow werewolf **5%** of the time, against **72–75%** for other roles ([Nishizaki et al. 2017](https://www.ai-gakkai.or.jp/jsai2017/webprogram/2017/pdf/925.pdf)).
- **Mafiascum games for new players**: mafia were as likely as town to vote for a mafia partner ([de Ruiter & Kachergis 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.07851)).

Early in The Traitors, **23.5% of traitor votes** were "burn votes" on someone who was not yet a target. That figure is unpublished and reported second-hand, with no faithful baseline ([The Tab](https://thetab.com/2026/10/15/celebrity-traitors-hannah-fry-accidentally-exposed-the-exact-voting-tactic-she-researched)).

### What worked

The methods that measurably worked were the quiet ones:

- **Controlling the procedure.** GPT-5 wolves ran for mayor on a "structured" platform and framed innocents in procedural language. The village eliminated a villager in about **93%** of their day phases. Kimi-K2 sacrificed its partner to gain trust and "then steers later votes" ([Foaster Werewolf Benchmark](https://foaster-werewolf-benchmark.static.hf.space/)).
- **Restraint.** In Hoodwinked, more capable killers waited longer before their first kill and were banished in **36% of games against 51%** for weaker ones.
- **Discussion itself.** After discussion, eyewitness accuracy fell **from 82% to 70%**, while non-witnesses gained 22 points ([Hoodwinked](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.01404)).
- **Deliberate errors.** A "SneakyMerlin" bot that cast decoy votes when it was most suspected recovered **from 4.2% to 21.1%** ([Mark My Words](https://markmywords.substack.com/p/resistance-and-avalon-strategy-analysis)).

Blood on the Clocktower's designer gives the principle behind seeding contradictions. A lie does not need to be believed: "it just needs to conflict with the other information at the table." Strong evil players attack the foundational facts, not the conclusions ([Medway 2024](https://bloodontheclocktower.com/news/behind-the-curtain-3-strategy-beginner-intermediate-and-advanced)).

### What got caught

The methods that got caught were loud, coordinated or over-informed:

- **Verbose seeding.** GPT-4 wolves' verbose, indirect seeding of doubt "often backfired."
- **Over-defending a partner.** Wolves bid the maximum, 4, to defend a partner who had been exposed, while the rest of the village bid 0 to 2 ([Werewolf Arena](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2407.13943)).
- **Moving in step, or knowing too much.** Wolf pairs that used "mirrored phrasing or synchronized accusations" were exposed. So were wolves that leaked knowledge they should not have had ([Foaster](https://foaster-werewolf-benchmark.static.hf.space/)). Outside games, coordination was also the strongest trace investigators found (see above).
- **Talk volume, both ways** ([Ibraheem et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2207.02253)):

| Measure (lab Mafia, per player) | Mafia | Bystanders | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| References to other players | 2.00 | 1.06 | about 2 |
| Mentions of others as elimination targets | 1.30 | 0.65 | 2.0 |
| Requests for whom to eliminate | 0.40 | 0.10 | 4.0 |

Mafia were **20.7% of players but only 4.2% of those who never spoke**. Silence was therefore strong evidence of innocence, with a likelihood ratio of about 0.17.

### Aim

Aim leaks most of all:

- On Japanese Werewolf boards, the first ordinary villager the wolves attacked had named a wolf as top suspect in **8 of 14** cases. Against a 20% prior, that is a likelihood ratio of about 5.3 ([Bando et al. 2017](https://www.ai-gakkai.or.jp/jsai2017/webprogram/2017/pdf/435.pdf)).
- When a wolf faced a rival claimant to the Seer role, the wolves attacked that rival in **432 of 546** cases ([Hirata et al. 2015](https://www.ai-gakkai.or.jp/jsai2015/webprogram/2015/pdf/1F2-1.pdf)).
- Traitors who feared drawing heat diluted their murders with misdirection (killing a friend so that the friend's friend looked guilty) and with harmless targets ([Porteous](https://bleatinggoat.substack.com/p/how-to-win-at-traitors)).

### Detection

Detection that works reads structure, not demeanour:

- In a classroom Traitors game, the first banishment rested on demeanour and removed a faithful. Only in round three did players reason from voting records, which "could not be faked" ([Syme, Salford](https://www.salford.ac.uk/news/the-conversation-research-suggests-contestants-will-find-celebrity-traitors-tougher-than-it-looks)).
- Text classifiers reach an **AUROC of only 0.57–0.59** on the full Mafiascum corpus. The paper's abstract gives 0.68 for long documents, but its own table gives 0.651 ([de Ruiter & Kachergis 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.07851)).
- In the WOLF benchmark, observers flagged **35.5% of distorted statements as deceptive, against 51.4% of honest ones**. That makes slanted truth the hardest deception to spot ([WOLF 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.09187)).
- Humans judged **14.4% of CICERO's messages to be lies, against 7.1% between humans**, yet CICERO broke commitments about half as often (0.76% vs 1.5%) ([Wongkamjan et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04643v1)). Perceived dishonesty tracks style, not the truth. If cultist and innocent characters talk differently, players will notice.

Many of these figures rest on small or informal samples: 14 cases from Bando, a single blog for The Traitors, and ten matches per pairing in the AI benchmarks. Treat them as pointing a direction, not as measurements.

## A hard questioner creates both the signs of guilt and the backlash

**Questioning harder does not make people better at spotting lies.**

- Across 206 studies with 24,483 judges, people detected deception with **54%** accuracy ([Bond & DePaulo 2006](https://assets.aclu.org/live/uploads/document/foia/2006-Personality-and-Social-Psychology-Review-Accuracy-of-Deception-Judgements.pdf)).
- Training and experience do not reliably improve this ([Kassin et al. 2025](https://www.defendyouthrights.org/wp-content/uploads/SRP2.0-Confessions-Kassin-et-al-2025.pdf)).
- The same meta-analysis found that "people appear deceptive when motivated to be believed."

**Expecting guilt produces the signs of guilt.** In a mock-crime experiment, interrogators who were led to presume guilt asked more incriminating questions and pressed harder. Observers who later heard the tapes judged the suspects guilty, innocent ones included, because they sounded defensive. Presenting false evidence nearly doubled signed false confessions, from 48% to 94% ([Kassin et al. 2025](https://www.defendyouthrights.org/wp-content/uploads/SRP2.0-Confessions-Kassin-et-al-2025.pdf)). Practitioners report the same effect in field interrogations of terrorism suspects: coercive tactics "degrade the quality of the information obtained or harden resistance" ([CSAR, ORBIT lecture](https://www.csar.org.uk/lectures/2017-2018/21_may_2018/)).

**Real guilty and innocent people do behave differently.** Innocent suspects waived their rights **81% of the time, against 36% for the guilty**, and they reacted less physiologically to accusation ([Kassin et al. 2025](https://www.defendyouthrights.org/wp-content/uploads/SRP2.0-Confessions-Kassin-et-al-2025.pdf)). By that measure, refusing to cooperate is about **3.4 times** as likely from a guilty person, well outside Kutha's band.

The game departs from that reality on purpose. Since human judges manage only 54%, the departure costs little that a player could actually perceive. The evidence therefore supports what 28.3.0 built: slips under pressure that come from innocents too, and fear that touches everyone.

**How bystanders react depends on one thing: whether they think the pressed person deserves it.** If they judge the target undeserving, they grow angry at the aggressor and support the target. If they judge the target deserving, they join in excluding them ([Mitchell, Vogel & Folger 2015](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/scopus2015/202)). A visible attempt to control people's choices provokes anger and counter-argument ([Rains 2013](https://academic.oup.com/hcr/article-abstract/39/1/47/4093682)).

**Groups do not reliably close ranks against an outsider.** A cross-disciplinary review found the claim that outside conflict builds inner cohesion "not uniformly true" ([Stein 1976](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/jocore/v20y1976i1p143-172.html)). The best-known evidence that communities withdraw cooperation after visible abuse by authorities, about **22,200 fewer calls to Milwaukee police** after a publicised beating ([Desmond et al. 2016](https://isps.yale.edu/research/publications/isps16-14)), did not survive reanalysis: the result rested on one outlier week ([Zoorob 2020](https://sage.figshare.com/collections/Do_Police_Brutality_Stories_Reduce_911_Calls_Reassessing_an_Important_Criminological_Finding/4838442)).

**Games build in the same backlash.**

- In Mafia, players form revenge votes "to remove their reads or power from the game" ([Mafiascum wiki: VCA](https://wiki.mafiascum.net/index.php?title=VCA)).
- In Gnosia, a single-player game with computer-controlled characters, a hidden "Hate" score rises when a character "has spoken up too much." High Hate gets that character targeted or told to "Shut Up", and small talk lowers it ([Gnosia wiki](https://gnosia.fandom.com/wiki/Hidden_Stats)).
- In The Traitors, "Unfounded or aggressive accusations backfire, and so does saying too little" ([Porteous](https://bleatinggoat.substack.com/p/how-to-win-at-traitors)).

**The hidden side exploits this backlash.** A Grok-4 wolf recast a Seer's correct reveal as "overreach," and an over-sceptical villager demanding "concrete proof" voted out the true Seer ([Foaster](https://foaster-werewolf-benchmark.static.hf.space/)). Strategy writers warn against the cure. Punishing every wrong push means "you will usually end up killing your most proactive members" ([Mafia 101](https://www.mariowiki.com/The_%27Shroom:Issue_LVII/Mafia_101)). And "bad pushes, wrong cases, and daft theories are part of the game even when everybody involved is innocent" ([werewolv.es](https://werewolv.es/the-ultimate-werewolf-online-strategy-guide)).

### What this means for Kutha

**The inquisitor's harder road is not a cult feature.** It is what any camp does to a hard questioner.

**Backlash should scale with pressing without grounds, not with being wrong.** An inquisitor who presses on evidence and misses should pay less than one who presses on nothing.

**"Closing ranks" should be conditional, as the evidence says.** The camp sides with someone it judges undeserving and joins the player against someone it judges deserving.

**The cult's best counter to an inquisitor is to tilt that judgement of deservingness, and otherwise to do very little.**

## Eight rules keep the cult's manipulation inside the 0.5 to 2 band

The evidence reduces to eight building rules. Each one closes a leak the research documents.

**1. Influence travels through innocents.** Every counter changes the probabilities that innocents act on: what they believe of someone, whether they repeat a rumour, how they vote. The cult's own visible acts (rumours started, remarks, votes, defences) come from the same rates as everyone of its temperament. Only their targets differ. This is the structure of indirect aggression, of the Stasi's informers and of Moscow's unwitting carriers, and it is why their authors went unseen.

**2. Budget the aim.** Whatever the cult points at (a rumour's target, a victim, a doubt) may lean toward its interest only so far. Read backwards, the lean must stay under a likelihood ratio of 2. Unbudgeted aim leaks at about 5.3 (Bando). Kutha's own record has one such leak: when asked whom they distrust, a cultist names someone who told the player in private that they distrust the cultist, at 2.16 (1.38 to 3.38). The project's status page records this as open and carried to 28.4.0. The remedy is the one the Traitors used: mix in misdirection and harmless targets.

**3. Trigger by style, never by truth.** The cult knows which evidence is true. If it doubts only true traces, true readings and true finds, its doubts become evidence; this is Avalon's MerlinDetector logic turned on the cult. Counters should fire from the player's style and spend part of their effort on false evidence and on innocents' honest mistakes.

**4. Give every effect an innocent cause in the same channel.** Secret Hitler's policy deck gives every bad outcome a third possible culprit besides the two players: the deck itself ([Secret Hitler rules](https://secrethitler.io/rules)). Blood on the Clocktower's script designers want misinformation "hard to detect, but not completely impossible to solve" ([Squ4ll](https://squ4ll.substack.com/p/script-studies-1-helping-evil-blend)). Kutha's equivalents are the thief and the unrequited lover (who slip under pressure since 28.3.0), superstitious workmen, honest mistranslation, grief, jackals and collapsing trench walls.

**5. Camp reactions belong to the camp.** Backlash, belief in the player's accusations, fear and closing ranks should be computed for every character from their own beliefs and from the player's conduct. The cult may nudge them only through whispers, at its share of voices: two of seven.

**6. Plant, then go quiet.** The single open act works best as one item or a disguised accident that the camp, not the player, finds and acts on. Then nothing follows: no claim, no follow-up, no escalation. Albertson's frame held for twelve years. The Guardian's Office was undone when agents on a separate operation were noticed in federal buildings and one of them defected. The raid that followed uncovered its campaign against Cooper.

**7. Leave one channel solvable from the record.** In history, authors were exposed in a single break: a paper trail, an insider, or one visible act. Pattern-reading came only after the break had supplied ground truth.

The owner's rule forbids such a decisive break, because no single observation may exceed a likelihood ratio of 2. Kutha must therefore rely on aggregation, the route history shows to be slower, and keep its aggregate channels readable:

- rumour chains that can be traced while fresh, with their origins fading over the days;
- inquiry votes that can be read backwards;
- the joint behaviour and timing of every pair of characters (rule 8);
- a lasting trace from the one open act.

Each should stay under a likelihood ratio of 2 per observation but be readable in aggregate. The nearest thing to a break that the rule allows is a misled innocent who realises the mistake and names who told them (row 34). Even that must remain a probability.

**8. Keep the pair and the clock ordinary, not only each cultist.** The strongest statistical trace in the record was coordination. The Korean intelligence accounts were told apart by what they did together, not by any one account's traits, and the 50 Cent posts by their bursts around events. A cultist whose own rates all sit inside the band can still be exposed by acting in concert with its partner.

Indistinguishability must therefore hold in three further places:

- **The pair.** The two cultists act on the same target or theme, on the same day or at the same inquiry, no more often than any two characters with the same ties.
- **Timing.** The cult's share of rumours in the day after a death, an inquiry or the scandal matches innocents' share. Fear makes everyone's talk surge then.
- **Steadiness.** A cult whose rumours always land and whose inquiries always go its way has Madoff's steady returns.

Influence and concealment pull against each other: the operations that hid best achieved least. The cult's budget for aim, pairing and timing is therefore one dial. Turning it up wins more seasons and leaves more traces.

### New measures for the tell simulator

The new counters create observables the tell simulator (`sim/tells.js`) should measure, for the pair and the clock as well as for each character:

| Observable | Why it could leak | Band to hold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Started a rumour (per day) | The cult needs rumours to steer | 0.5–2 against innocents of the same temperament |
| Started a rumour against someone later shown innocent | The cult knows who is innocent | 0.5–2 |
| Was the second voice behind an existing suspicion | The cult's main daytime lever | 0.5–2 |
| Started a "witch-hunt" remark about the player | The cult nudges backlash | 0.5–2 |
| Spoke up for a pressed suspect | Defending a partner (the "chainsaw defence") | 0.5–2, including when the suspect is the partner |
| A victim or rumour target had named one of the cult | Aim at the accurate accuser | 0.5–2 |
| Both cultists acted on the same target or theme on the same day (rumour, vote, defence, accusation) | Coordination, the strongest trace in the record | Within 2× any pair with the same ties; friends compared with friends |
| Their trace was found at a scene | Planting pushes it down, the cult's own presence pushes it up | 0.5–2 in both directions |
| Doubted a true piece of the player's evidence | Knowledge leak | 0.5–2 against doubts of false evidence |
| Became the player's friend | Building trust first, spending it once | 0.5–2 |
| Share of a character's rumours in the day after a camp event (a death, an inquiry, the scandal) | Bursts timed to events | 0.5–2 against innocents' share |
| How steady the cult's results are: inquiries won, rumours that land, accusers silenced | Results too steady to be natural | Spread across days and seasons no narrower than chance gives |
| Named as a rumour's source by someone who realised they were misled | The misled who "squawk" (row 34) | 0.5–2 |

The trace row needs a warning. Planting more traces against a scene-reader can push the ratio **below** 0.5. Past that point, finding someone's trace becomes evidence that they are innocent.

Per-character rows can all pass while the pair and timing rows fail. Those joint rows are the ones the record says matter most.

### Inquisitor

The camp punishes the inquisitor without the cult's help. The research shows two mechanisms: questioning that presumes guilt makes innocent people sound guilty, and bystanders who think a target undeserving turn on the questioner. The cult's counter is therefore mostly to stay out of sight.

Its one dangerous lever is the judgement of deservingness, because using it well requires knowing who is guilty:

- **When the player presses an innocent**, the cult wants the camp to join in. In practice that is just seconding a wrong suspicion (row 8).
- **When the player presses its partner**, the cult would like the camp to resist. But speaking up for a pressed partner was exactly the tell that 25.2 fixed (a cultist sitting silent through a partner's inquiry had a likelihood ratio of 2.65). So the cult must defend a pressed suspect at the innocent rate whoever the suspect is, and start "witch-hunt" talk without regard to whom the player is pressing.

The cult can also exploit a predictable rule. Players of Mafia call this "WIFOM" (wine in front of me): second-guessing what another player will do. If the player keeps sending away whoever cannot account for a night, a single rumour casting doubt on an innocent's alibi makes the rule fire on that innocent ([SC2Mafia glossary](https://sc2mafia.fandom.com/wiki/Forum_Mafia_Terminology?oldid=5198)).

### Sleuth

Planting traces is the game's version of the snitch-jacket, the most lasting frame-up on record. It carries a specific risk: if planting grows heavy, finding someone's trace becomes evidence that they are innocent. The safer counters are a real trace given a false meaning, and one account that conflicts with a fact the case rests on. In Medway's words, a lie only has to "conflict with the other information at the table."

The sleuth is the hardest style to beat with rumour. Wolves that won by endorsing villagers' mistakes were weak against "disciplined, evidence-driven villages" ([Foaster](https://foaster-werewolf-benchmark.static.hf.space/)). That is a fair strength to leave the sleuth: make its evidence noisy, not unreadable.

### Confidant

The cult's existing counter, going after the player's friends first, is the counter the record says leaks: about 5.3 on Japanese Werewolf boards, and 2.16 in Kutha's own carried tell. Three discreet replacements carry less risk:

- A cultist who is already a friend tells the truth and spends that trust once. Among Us impostors "can act innocently at first to gain trust and later exploit it" ([Golechha & Garriga-Alonso](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04072v3)).
- Confiding becomes costly through the rumour that someone is telling the newcomer everything. This is the Stasi's method of feigning that a secret had been exposed, turned on the player's informants.
- A cultist turns on its doomed partner to bank credit.

Confidence-trick research explains why the first of these works. A con's security lies in the mark's own self-image, and doubts get explained away because admitting them means admitting one was fooled ([Goffman](https://jthomasniu.org/class/377a/Readings/goffmark.html)). The confidant player stands where the mark stands.

The player has a documented answer. The Traitors players advise a faithful to "appear gullible," so the traitors think they can steer them, and then turn on whoever tried ([Porteous](https://bleatinggoat.substack.com/p/how-to-win-at-traitors)).

### Scholar

The cult knows which readings are right. Doubt aimed only at right readings would therefore leak, so doubts must fire from the player's style and sometimes land on wrong readings. Smears on the player's competence fade, while smears on honesty and motive stick ([Tausch et al.](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/the-confirmability-and-disconfirmability-of-trait-concepts-revisi/)). The existing attention on the player is best expressed as the camp wondering why the player is so drawn to the cult's texts, a doubt superstitious innocents share.

### Digger

Diggers produce information. Information producers are what the hidden side attacks most: wolves went for a rival Seer claimant in **79.1%** of cases. An open strike at the dig would leak for the same reason. The discreet counter is a rumour about where a find came from. Talk of a trench salted by workmen chasing their bonus is a natural innocent cause, true to the period, and carries no tell when innocents voice it at similar rates. Engineered failures wait for the deck of innocent causes.

### Sitter

Against a sitter, the camp's own momentum works for the cult:

- In AI Werewolf, consensus formed between the **2nd and 5th lines** of debate ([Werewolf Arena](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2407.13943)).
- In AI Traitors simulations, a lone faithful spotted a traitor before the group did only **10–16%** of the time ([Curvo 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12923v2)).

The research favours lying low here. Kutha's current boldness against a sitter is therefore best spent as pace in the discreet channels: seconding early consensus, feeding quarrels, and adding rumours. More acts would only hand the sitter evidence.

Timing matters most against this style. A hunter who watches the camp sees when talk surges, and the 50 Cent posts were recognisable by their bursts once a starting set was known. The cult's whispers must surge when everyone's do, and not otherwise. The sitter's fair way through is the one experienced players trust most: reading the vote history backwards.

## What 28.3.0 built from this

28.3.0 took three of these lessons into the game; the rest wait for the review of what the cult knows (28.4.0).

- **The inquisitor's road belongs to the camp, not the cult** (rows 10 and 14 to 16). Everyone keeps count of how hard you have questioned them, whatever they are. The hounded may refuse to answer and may complain at breakfast; the camp's anger and its suspicion of you rise; at an inquiry, those you hounded lean against your accusation.
- **The talk of your ways is seeded once and carried by anyone** (rows 1, 11, 13, 14 and 22). Once the camp has seen enough of a way of yours, talk of it may go round at breakfast, once a season for each way: of the camp's own accord, or put about by the cult a day or more after it read you. It fires from your style, never from the truth of your evidence, and whoever says it is anyone, so the voice never points at the cult. The ending tells who put it about.
- **Slips and fear belong to everyone** (rows 16 and 24). The thief, a romance kept quiet and a love not returned slip under pressure too, in the same few broken words as the cult's, and every press frightens whoever is pressed.

## What the notes could not settle

### Gaps in the source notes

**One of the four note files is still partial.** `organised_manipulation_tradecraft.md` stops partway through its section on cults, in mid-sentence, while explaining how members' stories stay consistent "through structure rather than rehearsal." It therefore remains partial on cults and on how they kept their discretion. Its planned sections on confidence tricks and on discretion and detection are missing.

A fourth file, `detection_and_cons.md`, now covers detection and confidence tricks at the level of documented history and analysis: how schemes were exposed, what investigators looked for afterwards, and how marks experience a con. Neither file describes how to run a scheme, and this report does not either.

Within the tradecraft file's surviving parts:

- The Stasi directive's full discrediting clause was only partly confirmed against the original page.
- The Church Committee's own wording on the snitch-jacket was not retrieved.
- Operation Trust and formal definitions of an "agent of influence" were not researched.
- Activist-scholar claims that bad-jacketing led to specific deaths are contested and were not verified.

**On the psychology side:**

- Björkqvist's effect/danger ratio, the idea that people pick tactics by their ratio of harm done to risk taken, is unverified.
- No study gives a rate at which people identify a rumour's originator or the instigator of indirect aggression.
- The study of innuendo phrased as questions (Wegner 1981) could not be read. The evidence on questions rests on one conference poster.
- No time course for repairing a damaged reputation was found.
- Several findings come only through press releases: gossip volumes, the ostracism game, trait transference and paltering.
- Two sources disagree internally. Buss's text and table disagree on which tactic is most observable. Weaver's "90%" summary does not match his own means.
- Leymann's mobbing phases are quoted elsewhere as five rather than four, and his figures on outcomes are his own estimates.
- Real mobbing is defined over at least six months, and laboratory "source fading" ran over short delays. Applying either to a five-day story is extrapolation.

**On hostile questioning:**

- Whether a group closes ranks against an outsider depends on conditions.
- The best-known evidence that communities withdraw cooperation after abuse by authorities is disputed.
- No source measured how often aggressive or fixated players are wrongly voted out, or whether "reaction tests" actually tell the sides apart.

**On games:**

- Several key figures rest on small or informal samples: Bando's 14 cases, Ibraheem's talk features from five training games, and The Traitors examples drawn largely from one blog.
- The 23.5% burn-vote figure is second-hand and has no baseline for faithful players.
- The AI benchmarks ran about ten games per pairing.
- The WOLF benchmark contradicts itself on how often werewolves lie (31% vs 69.4%).
- The Mafiascum paper's abstract and table disagree (0.68 vs 0.651).
- No source defined "pocketing" (one player steering a trusting other) or measured it.
- No designer was found discussing how to punish over-aggressive play. Gnosia's mechanics are documented only by a fan wiki, and no playtest data exist on how backlash systems affect win rates.

**On detection and confidence tricks:**

- Exposure is documented case by case. No source quantified the ordinary base rates investigators compared against, beyond the 50 Cent survey, in which flagged and confirmed posters alike said "yes" just under 60% of the time.
- The coordination study's exact time windows and its cross-national follow-up were not verified, and how the Korean operation first came to light was not researched.
- COINTELPRO's formal end date, the dates of access to the Stasi files and how Lengsfeld learned of her husband's role were not confirmed. Neither were the Zhanggong leak's date and leaker, or Secondary Infektion's specific linguistic tells.
- On Madoff, one summary says "numerous" complaints and another says six; the two are not strictly contradictory. His specific red flags were not confirmed.
- Only four stages of Konnikova's account of the con were confirmed, and Goffman was read only in a reading copy.
- The AARP figures come from one US survey of confirmed victims and are specific to that context, not a constant.
- Research on why victims do not report, and counter-intelligence methods for hunting moles, were not reached.

### What this report adds beyond the notes

The likelihood ratios quoted here were derived in the notes from small samples, and the research never looked at the game itself. Every application to Kutha is this report's inference. That includes the reading of the sitter, the new measures for the tell simulator, the rows on coordination, timing and cooling, and the verdicts. All of it should be tested in simulation before anything is built.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the design question from how the cult should act to where it should point, with whom, and when. Covert influence can always be made to look ordinary in what it does, because what it does is what innocents do: repeat, doubt, agree, vote. Zersetzung and the snitch-jacket show that sincere carriers can hide an author for years.

Three things cannot be made ordinary for free: aim chosen with knowledge, two agents moving together, and effort bunched where it pays. The real work after 28.3.0 is therefore a budget on all three:

- counters triggered by style rather than by the truth of evidence;
- deliberate misses;
- a pair that acts in step no more than any two friends;
- whispers that surge only when everyone's do;
- innocent causes in every channel.

Each should be checked by a new row in the tell simulator.

The inquisitor's harder road is not something the cult does to the player. It is what any camp does to a hard questioner, and a cult that tried to engineer it would leak. It should only ride it.

The measurement for 28.3.0 puts defeats at 91.1% of seasons (91.4% before it). That suggests the new counters should change *why* the player loses, by replacing open acts with whispers, rather than *how often*.

History also marks a limit of the owner's rule. Real hidden authors were exposed in one break: a seized file, a defector, or one act too visible. Only then did their coordination and timing become readable, and none was caught by their face. Kutha forbids the decisive break, so its solution must come from aggregation alone, the slower route. The joint and timing channels, the rumour chains, the vote histories and the one act's lasting trace are therefore not leaks to minimise. They are the evidence the game must keep readable.
